# map-view-2d

The 2D map engine of the Map Pane (task T-019, brief: `docs/plugins/briefs/map-view-2d.md`). Web part only. The only plugin that uses OpenLayers and the only one that registers proj4 projections.

**State:** on `main`: T-019, the selection highlight (owner, 2026-10-09), the drag fix, T-032 (no double-click zoom while placing or dragging), the review remarks N3–N6, and T-042 (SDK v1, M1): vector backgrounds, area of interest and clusters, fading of expired objects, settings changes at runtime, with the T-039 checks. With SDK 1.1.0 (M2, T-054 in parts): hatched fills (part A), the data model 1.1.0 shapes (part B: ellipses, arrowheads, route markers, shape labels), the drawing and editing service (part C), selection on the map (part D), the right-click menu with Centre map, Centre on and Zoom to (part E), dim, the setup share and the map type members (part F1), and the look (part F2). With SDK 1.2.0 (M3): the draw order of T-087 and the cancel signal of CR-0020 on the integration branch; workspaces, View's layers as a reference and fade lists (T-085) on its own branch. Acceptance criteria and what is left for the ITA's browser tests: `docs/handover/pca-map-view-2d.md`.

## What it does

- Fills the Map Pane with an OpenLayers map: pan, zoom (wheel, buttons, double-click; no double-click zoom while a point is picked or an object dragged, T-032), scale line, attribution of the background map.
- Draws the whole live picture: one WebGL layer per emc2 layer in `drawOrder` order; `core.symbol` objects as images from the SymbolService (from a shared sprite atlas, with the direction of movement as a separate arrow); `core.shape` points, lines, polygons and circles with their style, edges densified as geodesics; anything else as a grey fallback marker. Changes are applied at most once per second per layer (`updateCadenceMs`).
- Up to `textThreshold` visible symbols, the texts of symbols with text amplifiers are drawn on top: the text alone, the symbol cut out, so the symbol itself stays on its layer and the layer order decides which objects lie on top (T-087; owner, 2026-10-22). Texts and shape labels lie above all objects.
- Hatched fills (data model 1.1.0 `fillPattern: 'hatch'`): 45° lines about 8 px apart in the fill colour and opacity, fixed on screen at every zoom (owner, 2026-10-16), with OpenLayers' own WebGL pattern fill. Solid and 1.0.0 areas are filled as before.
- Ellipses as ellipses (brief function 18): like circles, a ring of geodesic points around the centre, at each bearing the ellipse's radius in that direction, turned by its rotation (degrees clockwise from true north); as an area it gets its style, the hatch and the highlight.
- **Arc bands and corridors** (M5, brief function 36, criteria 84 to 88; PCA-gmti's finding, owner JX, 2026-11-07): a `core.shape` with an `ArcBand` or a `Corridor` is drawn as an area with its style, as a polygon is, not as the fallback marker (`core/areas.ts`). An `ArcBand` is the area between `innerRadius` and `outerRadius` round `center`, clockwise from `startBearing` to `endBearing`: the arcs are geodesic circles (drawn within 0.5 m of the circle), the sides geodesic lines along the bearings (a point every 4 km, so within 0.2 m), a radius of 0 is a sector from the centre, 300° to 60° goes across north, equal bearings give the whole ring (a ring with a hole, a disc for radius 0). A `Corridor` is the area within `width` / 2 of the centre line, measured geodesically (the sides follow the geodesic every 4 km), with flat ends square to the first and last segments, round joins on the outside of a turn and the two sides cut where they cross on the inside (a sharper turn within a width leaves a small loop, filled as any self-crossing outline is). Labels, hatch, highlight, fade, the budget and the pick (outline only) are the polygon's. A band or corridor with no area (no width, a radius that is not a number) keeps the marker. A drag previews either as a marker at the pointer (function 26); `edit` does not reshape them. The areas, and every other shape, go on past the edge of the world in EPSG:3857 when they cross 180° (one short area, the next copy of the world), and the pick looks in the neighbouring copies too. Known limit: an area that encloses a pole is not drawn as one that does.
- **Display settings** (M5, brief function 35, criteria 77 to 83; ADR-0046, SDK 1.4.0 part 5, owner Q13 and JF, 2026-11-07): the map view reads `ctx.display`, follows `onChange` at once, and never writes it. Every `render` call carries `size` (the user's `symbolSize`; the map view has no symbol size setting of its own: it went at the 1.4.0 freeze) and, from SDK 1.8.0, no labels (`labels: []`, see the next bullet); a new size makes the symbol images again (the atlas starts over), without a reload or a new subscription. The labels are placed by the map view itself, **by place** (next bullet). With a speed vector length above 0 each symbol with a `speed` above 0 (m/s, modifier `speed`) and a `direction` gets a line from its position along the direction, `speed` × seconds metres long and geodesic (`core/vectors.ts`), in black for every identity (owner, LB, 2026-11-08), 2 px, under the symbols, faded with its object, never picked, short across the antimeridian. Where a symbol has a vector, the map view's own direction arrow is not drawn (LB); any other symbol keeps its arrow (owner, LC, 2026-11-08), unless the user has switched the arrow off (SDK 1.8.0, `directionArrow`, below). Without `ctx.display` (a shell older than part 5) the setting `symbolSize` is used and no label is placed.
- **The speed vector in steps** (M6, brief function 38, criteria 94 and 95; T-164, SDK 1.5.0 part 3, owner MZ A and Q5 A): the length is `ctx.display.speedVectorSeconds`, one of 5, 10, 15, 30 s, 1, 2, 5, 10, 20 and 30 min, or 0 for none, that many seconds of travel at the object's `speed` (`speedVectorSecondsOf` in `core/vectors.ts`). The shell always fills it (its `resolveDisplay`), and the deprecated `speedVectorMinutes` is not read. The shell has made a stored length one of the steps, so the map view rounds nothing; a change draws every vector again at once, with no new subscription. Colour, order, fading, no pick, the missing arrow and the antimeridian are as before. The window with the steps is the shell's.
- **Labels by place and the direction arrow switch** (M8, brief function 35; SDK 1.8.0, CR-0043 part A; T-163, owner A): the user gives each of five places round a symbol (top left, top right, bottom left, bottom right, below) a label or none, and switches the direction arrow on or off. **The map view draws every placed label as text at its place**, from the object (`core/labelPlaces.ts`): `name` from `properties.name`, `speed` in km/h as `symbology` writes it (metres per second times 3.6, rounded), and `uniqueDesignation`, `higherFormation`, `additionalInformation`, `staffComments`, `type`, `dtg` and `altitudeDepth` from the amplifier of that name in `symbol.modifiers` (a label the object has none of draws nothing). A corner label lies beside the image with its top or bottom edge on the image's, the text growing away from it; the one below is centred under it (`placedLabelStyle` in `engine/styles.ts`, from the image's real size in the atlas, a marker's radius otherwise), in the look's label colours on its halo, faded with its object, counted with the symbol against the text threshold (function 6). **`symbology` is asked with `labels: []`** for every image, so it draws none of the labels that can be placed, and still the amplifiers off that list at their APP-6 places (country, quantity and the like); the text image that carries those is asked for only where the symbol has such an amplifier (`hasOffListText`). A change of places redraws the texts only: the images do not depend on them, so the atlas is not started again. **The arrow follows the switch** (`directionArrow`): a symbol with a direction has it only while the switch is on, as before not where a speed vector is drawn, and a drag preview follows too. The places and the arrow come from `ctx.display.labelPlaces` and `directionArrow`, required since the 1.8.0 freeze; the map view does not read the deprecated `labels` any more (its fallback was removed at the freeze), and a setting stored under 1.7.0 reaches it already converted, with the arrow on (owner WZ A). **The gap between an image and a label is `LABEL_GAP`, 7 px** (RC1, owner YC A): the reach of the selection frame beyond the image (its 3 px gap and half of its 5 px casing, `HIGHLIGHT_REACH`) and half of the label's halo, so that the frame of a selected symbol never covers the first characters of a label (it hid "5 " of "5 INF" and "3" of "36 km/h" at 2 px); the labels do not move when the symbol is selected, and the gap is the same at every symbol size and for every image, since the frame follows the image's real size.
- **Vessel symbols: the hull in 10° steps, and no blank frame when the atlas grows** (M8, brief function 33 and criterion 108; T-200, owner TY B and TZ A): a `core.symbol` with `standard` `vessel` is drawn from the service's image, which has the hull turned by `direction` (and a mark when the vessel does not move). **The hull is in steps of 10°** (`vesselHeading`, `steppedSymbol` in `core/drawData.ts`): `direction` goes to the nearest step, a half step up (34 gives 30, 35 and 36 give 40, 355 and above 0); that value is in the image's key (`appearanceKey`) and in the request to `symbology`, with text and without, and in the key of the text image, so a turning ship changes its image every 10° and a hull look has at most 36 images. The object keeps its exact `direction`: its properties and its speed vector read it unchanged. **No blank frame when the atlas grows** (`engine/olEngine.ts` `requestSwap`, `applySwap`, `engine/layerSwap.ts`, `engine/atlas.ts` `loadImages`): in OpenLayers 10.10 `setStyle` on a WebGL layer in use throws its renderer away, and the new one draws nothing until its buffers are built in a worker, so every new image in the atlas blanked all the symbols. Now a new image goes into the atlas being built, and the style the layers show is changed **at most four times a second** (`STYLE_SWAP_INTERVAL_MS`), with the images that came in between: the atlas image and the style's other images are loaded and decoded first (for at most `IMAGE_LOAD_TIMEOUT_MS`, 3 s); then, on a map that is shown, **a second WebGL layer per emc2 layer with the new style is added next to the one in use** (same source, same WebGL canvas), and when every new layer has its buffers (OpenLayers builds them only while the view is not moving, so a swap waits for a pan or an animation to end, and is given up and tried again after 600 frames without them) and has drawn a frame, the old ones are taken away (`LayerSwap`); a map that is not shown has the style set at once. Until the swap a symbol whose image is not in the shown atlas yet is not drawn if it is new, and keeps the image it had if it changed (`P.inAtlas` 2 and `promotePending`); a symbol that waits is not picked either (its text image is drawn already); an atlas that is started again (a new symbol size, other labels) draws nothing of its symbols until its first swap; a tactical graphic that brings a new dash pattern is drawn with the old style until the next swap. Measured in the browser (no GPU, 320 ships, the atlas growing from 36 to 260 images in 30 s, symbol pixels counted in the layers' WebGL canvas every 50 ms): before, every swap was followed by a frame with no symbol drawn; after, 20 swaps and none (the only empty samples were when the playback added its own layer, which makes OpenLayers rebuild the canvas group: not a swap). A swap draws the symbols of the old and the new layer in one frame, so a translucent symbol is a little darker for that frame. A swap makes the browser build the buffers of every layer twice for a moment (not measured with 10 000 objects).
- **Sensor reports** (M6, brief function 39, criteria 96 to 100; T-151, SDK 1.5.0 part 3, owner Q41 to Q44): an object whose data type declares `sensorReport: true` (`ctx.dataTypes`; today `gmti.detection`) is drawn with `SymbolService.sensorReportSymbol()`, the admin's image as it comes, its anchor at the position, instead of its APP-6 symbol. No `render` call is made for it; the user's symbol size does not change it; it has no name, no direction arrow, no speed vector and no text, so it does not count toward the text threshold. In every other way it is a symbol: picked by a click or box on its image, highlighted with a frame round the image, faded by its data type, in its layer's order, one object in the clusters, and with its `gmti.pairing` line. The image is asked for at every draw and once a second while sensor reports are drawn; another image (another `image`, size or anchor) draws the symbols in view again within a second, so a settings change shows without a reload. A service without `sensorReportSymbol` (an older `symbology`), one that throws (logged), a data type without the flag and an unknown data type give the APP-6 symbol as before. A change of a data type's flag draws its objects again at once. The object carries `sensorReport: true` in its draw item (`core/drawData.ts`); the engine decides whether the image is there (`sensorReportEntry` in `engine/olEngine.ts`), the atlas key has a generation number.
- **Relation lines** (M5, brief function 34, criteria 72 to 76; ADR-0051 point 6, ADR-0052): for an object with `relations` whose kind a loaded plugin declares (`ctx.relationKinds`) and the user has switched on (`ctx.display.relations`), a geodesic line from the object's drawn position to the other object's, in the kind's colour, width, dash and arrowhead (`core/relations.ts`). A point object is drawn at its point, any other where its label goes. One line when both objects relate to each other in one kind, none for a zero-length line, an undeclared kind or an object that is not drawn; the line has the opacity of its carrying object (function 42, below). It is a line item with `aux` in the carrying object's layer, drawn under the graphics and the symbols, never picked or labelled; it is drawn again, added or removed only where it differs, after each redraw, so that a moving platform draws its lines again, not all of them every time.
- **Relation lines with one end off screen** (M7, brief function 41, criterion 102; T-183, owner Q59 B, no contract change): a relation of a held object whose other object the map does not hold (it lies outside the subscription's area) still gets its line. `core/farEnds.ts` fetches such an object with `ctx.objects.get` (at most `MAX_FETCH_IDS` ids per call) as soon as a relation first names it, and again every 2 s while a line to it is wanted, so the line follows the far end at most about 2 s late. It is forgotten when no held object names it any more, and an id the fetch does not return (deleted, or not visible to the user) draws no line. A platform that leaves the area goes on as a far end at once, from the object the map held, so its line does not go while the fetch is on its way. At most 1 000 far ends are held; the lines to others are not drawn, and a warning is logged once. The far end is only the end of the line: it is not drawn, picked or selected, and when it comes into view the subscription brings it as usual. Without `ctx.objects` (an older shell) there are no far ends. Only relations that would be drawn are fetched (a declared kind, switched on, from a carrier in a layer that is drawn).
- **Relation lines fade with their carrier** (M7, brief function 42, criterion 103; T-186): a line has the opacity of the object whose `relations` hold the entry (for `gmti.pairing` the detection), at every frame: it fades when that object expires (function 13) or is on a fade list (function 24), and goes when it goes. The other end's fading does not fade it (M7-S8), and there is no setting of its own. For a pair that carry the relation both ways the line takes the higher opacity, so it fades only when both do (`core/relations.ts`).
- **Relation kinds' colours from settings** (M7, brief function 43, criterion 104; T-181, SDK 1.6.0 part C, owner QM A): a line takes its kind's line from `ctx.relationLines` (the shell resolves the colour from the declaring plugin's setting, `gmti.pairing` from `gmti`'s `pairingColour`, and again after each settings change), not from `ctx.relationKinds`; a kind it does not list, or a shell without `ctx.relationLines`, gives the manifest's line. Width, dash and arrowhead stay the manifest's. On a change the lines are drawn again at once (`MapController.setRelationLines`), without a reload or a new subscription; the line's draw key holds its style, so only lines whose colour changed are redrawn.
- **Display classes** (M7, brief function 40, criterion 101; T-182, SDK 1.6.0 part D, owner QN B and Q58 A): the user's switches `ctx.display.classes` ("Dwell areas" `dwell-area`, "Sensor coverage" `sensor-coverage`, on by default; `classShown`) hide every object whose `displayClass` is switched off, and every overlay item of that `displayClass` in any plugin's overlay. A hidden object is not given to the engine, so it is not drawn, picked, box-selected or highlighted, as an object of a locally hidden layer; the selection keeps its id, and `edit` and `append` refuse it as an object not on the map. Nothing is deleted: the relation lines of other objects stay, and a hidden object carries none. A switch's change draws the objects and overlay items of that class again or takes them off the map at once (`applyClasses`), with no new subscription; an object that arrives while its class is off is drawn when it is switched on. Overlay items keep their index as id, so the others do not move. The switches themselves are the shell's (Display settings window); the map view reads them and never writes them.
- **Append: continue a line** (M7, brief function 44, criterion 105; T-176, SDK 1.6.0 part B, owner QG A and QH A): `MapView.append(id, { style?, signal? })` lets the user continue a held `LineString` from its last point. It is OpenLayers' `Draw.extend` on the line's points (`startDrawing('line', …, { extend })`), so the preview, the clicks, the cursor and the finish (double-click on the last point, Enter) are those of drawing a line (function 18), with the line itself drawn in `style` (the object's own without one) and highlighted while it runs, selected or not. Resolves with the line's points exactly as they were, then the added ones (`appendedLine` in `core/drawing.ts`; a point twice in a row is one), or `null` when none was added. Backspace and a right click take back the last **added** point only (the engine counts them: `added()`), and a right click with none added is Escape; Escape, an abort, a workspace switch, another interaction and the line being deleted give `null`. It rejects at once with `NotOnMapError` for an object the map does not draw (not held, a hidden layer or display class, outside the projection, or a point the projection cannot show) and with `NotALineError` for any other geometry; an already aborted signal resolves `null` first. It changes nothing: the caller sends the update. No right-click menu while it runs (function 27). **A double-click that ends it (or any drawing or edit the user finishes) leaves the selection alone (owner, SF A, 2026-11-12):** OpenLayers ends a drawing on the second click's release and then sends that click, which on empty map would have cleared the selection and closed draw-shapes' Edit panel; for `FINISH_CLICK_GRACE_MS` (50 ms) after a finish the map view takes a click as that last click, not as a click on the map (`MapController.justFinished`). Enter and Escape never sent one.
- **Text objects** (M7, brief function 45, criterion 106; `core.text`, data model 1.6.0 part A, ADR-0055 point 4): a `core.text` with a point and a `text.value` is a draw item `textobject` (`core/drawData.ts`): its text centred on its point, turned `text.rotation` degrees clockwise from **true north** at the point (the engine adds where north points on the screen, so it is upright at 0 in a north-up map and follows north in a polar projection), `text.size` pixels (default 14, `TEXT_SIZE_DEFAULT`), in `style.strokeColor` or else the look's text colour, with the label halo, line breaks as new lines. It is an item of its layer like any other: the WebGL layer cannot draw text, so a layer that holds text objects gets a canvas text layer over its own (z-index 0.5 above it, under the next layer's), made when the first text object arrives and removed with the last (`syncTextLayer` in `engine/olEngine.ts`); it is in the layer's feature source, so the layer's visibility, fading (`FADED_OPACITY` on the colour and the halo), the clusters' and budget counts (one object) and the display class are the object's. Always drawn at every zoom: function 6's text threshold is for symbol texts and labels and does not look at it. **Picked and box-selected on its text's box**, turned with the text (a new `quad` candidate in `core/pick.ts`: hit anywhere inside, within the tolerance outside; a selection box that holds its position, crosses its edge or lies wholly inside it takes it), and before the other items of its layer, as it lies over them. The box is the text as the browser draws it (SN A, owner, 2026-11-12): its width is the widest line measured with the canvas' `measureText` in the very font the text is drawn in (`textObjectFont`: weight, size, the look's font), so a long, narrow, wide or non-ASCII text has the box it is drawn in; lines are 1.2 of the size tall (`textBox`). The measured widths are kept per font and line in `engine/textMeter.ts` (at most 2 000, then it starts over) and measured again when the look's font changes or a font finishes loading; a browser with no 2D canvas gets the old guess (0.6 of the size a character). Every text object of a layer is a pick and box-select candidate whatever the distance of its point, because a long text's box reaches further than the query's (`candidatesIn`); the pointer is tested against the box. Selected, it gets a frame round the box turned with the text; `dragObject` previews the text and returns the drop position; `edit` moves its point only (a `Point`). A `core.text` with no text, or not on a point, keeps the fallback marker. The drawing is not tested in a real browser's pixels by the unit tests (jsdom): see the handover.
- **Overlay areas and labels** (SDK 1.4.0 part 6, ADR-0049 point 8): an overlay's `area` is drawn as a polygon with its holes and its style (outline, fill, hatch), geodesic edges, in every projection and across the antimeridian; a `label` as centred text on a halo in the look's colours, with the plugin's colour, size (8 to 32 px) and halo colour when it gives them. `overlayProblem` decides before anything is drawn: an overlay it refuses is not drawn at all until the next `set`, and the reason is logged.
- Lines with `arrowhead: 'end'` get an arrowhead at their last point, turned along the last segment as drawn and in the line's colour; lines with `pointMarkers: 'circle'` get small circles filled in the line's colour at their own points, as data model 1.1.0 describes them (owner, 2026-10-16) (not at the points added for geodesic edges). Both sizes follow the line width with a minimum: the arrowhead 4× the width, at least 12 px; a marker the width + 2 px, at least 4 px (owner, 2026-10-16). They fade, hide and go with their line, and a click on them picks the line. On the Canvas paths (other plugins' overlays, the drag preview) the same, with the hatch as a pattern.
- A shape's `properties.name` is drawn as its label: at an area's interior point, at the middle of a line, in the look's text colour on a halo in its ground colour (brief function 21). Labels follow the text threshold with one count for visible symbols and named shapes (owner, 2026-10-15 and 2026-10-16): above it no texts are drawn at all. Shape labels do not need the symbology plugin.
- Shows a background map from a `BackgroundMapProvider` that supports the projection, or a graticule on a neutral background. After a projection switch it takes the user's current choice if it fits, else the user's last choice in that projection, else the first available in `preferredBackgrounds`, else the first available (brief function 3); the choice per projection is kept in the browser (`localStorage`, `emc2.map-view-2d.backgrounds`). A background that goes while shown (its provider removed) is replaced as after a switch, with the notice "The background map X is not available; showing Y."
- Draws WMS backgrounds (SDK 1.3.0 `WmsSource`, T-072; brief function 3, ADR-0035) in every configured projection a provider lists, the polar ones included, matched by their codes and never redrawn in another one:
  - **tiles** (default): an OpenLayers `XYZ` source with a grid made from the projection's extent (one tile at the top level in a square extent, two in EPSG:4326, `tileSize` pixels each, 22 levels), the world repeating sideways where the projection does; **or one image per view** (`mode: 'image'`): the view and half of it more around, cut at the extent, at the view's resolution, at most 4 096 pixels a side (a larger view asks for a smaller image, drawn scaled up);
  - the request URL is built by the map view (`core/wms.ts`), the same parameters as the test kit's `wmsGetMapUrl`, each once and nothing else; EPSG:4326 in WMS 1.3.0 is latitude first, with `CRS`, and `SRS` in 1.1.1; a box never goes beyond the extent. OpenLayers' own WMS sources are not used because they take the axis order from the projection object and ask for fractional sizes, which the SDK's contract excludes;
  - checked before it is offered, like a tile source: the URL on the emc2 server, at least one layer, a known version and format, `tileSize` from 64 to 1 024, a list of projections (never `'any'`); what is wrong goes to the log once;
  - a projection whose extent cannot be cut into tiles inside it (its sides not in a whole-number ratio up to 16 to 1) gets WMS backgrounds in `'image'` mode only;
  - an image that fails leaves its area empty.
- Draws vector backgrounds (SDK v1 `VectorSource`, e.g. Natural Earth) in every configured projection, the polar ones included: clipped to the projection's `worldExtent` in lon/lat, densified to 1° steps (edges straight in lon/lat), projected once per projection, with the zero-width slits of polygons around a pole or cut at ±180° removed. `load()` is called once, when the background is first chosen; a failed load shows "Background unavailable" in the Map Pane controls (brief function 11).
- Subscribes to the live picture of the area in view with the object budget of its rendering mode (`{ area: 'viewport', budget }`). Objects that leave the area go from the map but stay selected (criterion 40); clusters are drawn as discs split by standard identity with their count, a locally hidden layer is subtracted at once, and a click on one zooms in two levels (brief function 12); a double-click zooms in only that once, without OpenLayers' double-click zoom on top (owner, 2026-10-13).
- Draws objects faded (40 %) from their expiry on when their data type's behaviour says `fadeWhenExpired`, checking the clock at the update cadence, and applies behaviour changes at once (brief function 13).
- **The fade in steps** (M7 RC2, brief function 46, criterion 107; T-192, CR-0042 option A; SDK 1.7.0): an object whose data type has a `fade` (`DataType.fade`, a `SteppedFade` the shell resolves from the declaring plugin's `fadeSetting`) is drawn at the SDK's `fadeOpacity(object, fade, now)`, counted from its `time.observed`: full until `fromSeconds`, then one step down every (`invisibleAtSeconds` − `fromSeconds`) × `stepPercent` / 100 s, **neither drawn nor picked from `invisibleAtSeconds`** (it stays held and selected, as with a display filter; ECore removes it). It takes the place of the expiry fade, so an expired object is not drawn at 40 % on top; on a fade list (function 24) it takes the lower of its step and the list's 40 %. An object without `time.observed` is not faded. The opacity is a draw item's `opacity` (`core/drawData.ts`, next to the old `faded`; `itemOpacity` in `core/expiry.ts` gives what an item is drawn at, symbol, label, vector and highlight alike); a relation line has its carrier's opacity (of a pair that carry the relation both ways, the higher), and none when the carrier is invisible. `ExpiryTracker` keeps the time of each object's next step (`nextFadeStep`), so the clock is checked at the update cadence, about once a second, and only the objects whose step has changed are drawn again. A changed fade (`ctx.onDataTypesChange`) applies at once, a longer one also bringing back an object that was invisible. Needs the shell's `ctx.dataTypes[].fade` (CDA, `cda/sdk-1.7`); the plugin's tests use the SDK test kit, which resolves it.
- Highlights the selected objects (`MapView.selection`, owner decision 2026-10-09): a yellow frame on a dark casing around a symbol's image, a halo around points and markers, a wide casing under lines and area outlines. It follows live updates and the drag preview, and shows only what the map draws. The "move" cursor shows over the object that can be dragged.
- Selects objects on the map (brief function 14, ADR-0032 point 1), for every kind of object; the selection stays the shared `MapView.selection`, which other plugins read and set:
  - a click on an object selects it and replaces the selection (the topmost, as `pick`); a click on empty map, or Escape, clears it;
  - Ctrl+click (Cmd+click on a Mac, where Ctrl+click opens the menu) adds the object, or removes it if it is selected; a click with other keys (Shift, Alt, Ctrl on a Mac) selects nothing;
  - Ctrl+drag (Cmd+drag on a Mac) draws a box and replaces the selection with the objects in it, also when it starts on an object; Shift+drag still zooms to a box. A symbol or point is in the box by its drawn position, a line or area by its outline: a box wholly inside an area does not select it (owner, 2026-10-16);
  - at most 1 000: a box keeps the first 1 000, topmost layer first, and a notice says how many were left out; a Ctrl/Cmd+click that would add the 1 001st adds nothing, with a notice; `selection.set` with more keeps the first 1 000 and logs a warning;
  - only what the map holds and shows: not objects inside a cluster (a box over clusters counts them in a notice that asks to zoom in), outside the area, or in a locally hidden layer. A click on a cluster zooms in, with or without Ctrl/Cmd (owner, 2026-10-16);
  - while `pickPoint`, a drawing or an edit runs, clicks belong to it and there is no box (owner, 2026-10-16); Escape cancels it first, and only the next Escape clears the selection. While `dragObject` waits, clicks and boxes still select, and one Escape ends the wait with `null` and clears the selection (owner, 2026-10-20);
  - only a delete removes an id from the selection: ids of objects the map does not hold (set by another plugin, e.g. `object-list`, or left the area, or missing from a new snapshot) stay (criterion 40).
- Map Pane controls (top right), collapsible (owner, 2026-10-21): they start as one icon button, "Map controls", which opens a box with the lists and the dim slider; its close button or Escape in it (the lists included, T-172) collapses it again. Open or closed is remembered per browser (`localStorage`), not in the setup. While collapsed, a problem the box would show puts a warning marker on the button and its text in the button's name. In the box: the projection (the configured ones; switching keeps the centre where possible and the metres per pixel) and the background map (only those for the current projection, or "None (grid)").
- Checks at start whether WebGL runs on a GPU (hardware, software, none), logs it and reports it through the plugin status. Without a GPU, a notice (top right) says so; the user can dismiss it. (The M0 budget warning is gone: in area mode the server keeps every view within the budget.) Without WebGL the Map Pane says the map cannot be drawn.
- Provides `MapView` (`emc2.map-view`) to other plugins: viewport, `goTo`, pointer events, pick, pick a point, drag an object (Escape cancels a running pick and a grabbed drag, but not while the user types in a text field; a drag that only waits ends with the Escape that clears the selection; the caller's cancel signal ends either, below), overlays, selection, active layer, local layer visibility, rendering mode. The drawing and editing service (brief function 17, T-054 parts A and C) changes nothing itself; the caller sends the change request:
  - Dragging a shape (brief function 26, T-066): `dragObject` drops a shape's first position (a circle's or an ellipse's centre) moved by the drag; the preview shifts every position of a line or polygon by the same difference in lon/lat (latitudes clamped at ±90, longitudes not wrapped), a line's own points too, so a route's point markers move with it.
  - The cancel signal (SDK 1.2.0 part 1, CR-0020): `pickPoint`, `dragObject`, `draw` and `edit` take an optional `signal`. An abort ends that interaction as Escape does (it resolves with `null`, the tools or the drag preview go) and nothing else: it never clears the selection, not even while a drag only waits. A signal already aborted resolves with `null` at once and starts nothing (it does not end another plugin's interaction); for `edit` it wins over `NotOnMapError`. An abort after the interaction ended, or after another replaced it, changes nothing.
  - `draw(kind, { style })` on OpenLayers' draw interaction, with a preview in the caller's style (a line with its arrowhead and markers). Circle, ellipse, square and rectangle by pressing and dragging; polygon and line point by point (a double-click or Enter finishes, Backspace removes the last point). Owner, 2026-10-16: an ellipse fits the dragged box with its axes along the screen (the rotation is then changed by editing); a square is upright from corner to corner, its side the longer drag length; a click where the projection cannot show a point sets none, and a dragged shape released there draws nothing (`null`, with a line in the log). It returns lon/lat: a circle with its radius and an ellipse with its axes in geodesic metres and its rotation, squares and rectangles as closed polygons. Repeated points are dropped; an area without three distinct points or any area, and a line without two, give `null`.
  - `edit(id, { style })` on OpenLayers' modify and translate interactions: drag inside the shape to move it, drag a polygon's or line's corners and points, a circle's radius handle, an ellipse's two axis handles (the minor end keeps the right angle; dragged past the major, the two swap). Enter returns the new geometry. A moved circle or ellipse keeps its radius, axes and rotation (only its centre moves); a moved polygon or line moves as on screen. Rejects at once with `NotOnMapError` for an object the map does not draw (not held, in a locally hidden layer, outside this projection) or with a point this projection cannot show, and refuses geometries it does not edit (only Polygon, LineString, Point, Circle and Ellipse).
  - Escape, another interaction, unmounting the map or (for `edit`) the object's deletion give `null`. One interaction at a time with `pickPoint` and `dragObject`; no double-click zoom while one runs. Enter and Backspace count on the page, in the map or on the button that started it, not in text fields or on other controls (owner, 2026-10-16).
- Right-click menu (brief function 15, ADR-0032 points 3 and 5), the shell's menu opened by the map view (`ctx.actions.openMenu`); the browser's own menu is never shown on the map. A Ctrl+click on a Mac is a right click there.
  - On a cluster or empty map: the map actions, with the position in lon/lat (none where the projection cannot show the point).
  - On a selected object (any selected one among the objects under the pointer): the object actions for the whole selection, also its ids the map does not hold.
  - On one object that is not selected: it becomes the selection first. On several that overlap and none selected: the shell lets the user choose one (topmost first) and selects it.
  - No menu while `pickPoint`, a drawing or an edit runs (owner, 2026-10-17); while `dragObject` waits, as usual.
- Own actions (brief function 16, ADR-0032 point 6), in the `view` group, needing no permission, with Lucide icons: **Centre map** (map action) centres on the position, keeping the zoom; **Centre on object(s)** and **Zoom to object(s)** (object actions, one object or several, every kind) call `MapView.centreOn` and `MapView.zoomTo`:
  - the objects come from the live picture, and those the map does not hold from the server (`ctx.objects.get`, 1 000 ids per call); a failed read counts them as missing, with a log line;
  - the extent is taken in the map's own coordinates over every position inside the projection's `worldExtent` (circles and ellipses by their outlines); where the map repeats sideways, it is the narrowest span holding every point (after the widest gap between them), so objects on both sides of the antimeridian are close together whatever their order;
  - Centre on centres on the extent's middle and keeps the zoom; Zoom to fits the extent with 10 % of the view free on each side, but never closer than a view `zoomToWidthMetres` wide (10 km), which is also the view for one object (owner, 2026-10-17);
  - both resolve with the ids they could not show (gone, or no position this projection can show); with none left the map stays where it is and a notice says so (criterion 42).
- The look (brief function 21, ADR-0034; owner, 2026-10-20): shape labels and the counts on clusters are written in the look's text colour on a 3 px halo in its ground colour, the labels in its text font and the counts in its data font (the label halo is 90 % opaque, so the map shows through faintly; the count's halo, on the cluster's disc, is opaque), and follow a change of look, light or dark at once (`ctx.theme`). Their sizes (12 px labels, 11 px counts) are the map view's own in every density, and the APP-6 affiliation colours of clusters, the selection highlight, the graticule and the background maps do not change with the look. The Map Pane controls are the SDK's shared components (`Select`, `Slider`, `Notice`, `Button`); the map's own elements (zoom buttons, scale line, attribution, the neutral background) take the `--emc2-…` tokens.
- Dim (brief function 19): a slider in the Map Pane controls (the SDK's `Slider`) darkens what lies under the objects towards black, in steps of 10 % from 0 to `highestDim` (90 %; owner, 2026-10-17): the background map, or with "None (grid)" the neutral background and graticule too (owner, 2026-10-17). It is a black layer of its own between the background and the object layers, so objects, labels, clusters and the highlight are never dimmed. A lower `highestDim` takes the level down at once.
- Map type (SDK 1.1.0, for setups and `bookmarks`): `mapType` is the projection and the background shown; `onMapTypeChange` hears every change of either, also the map's own fallback after a projection switch; `setMapType` switches both and returns what it applied. A projection that is not configured keeps the current one, and a background that is not available falls back as in function 3, each with a notice; the background stays the choice in that projection, so it is shown when its provider comes. `onLayerVisibilityChange` hears every local visibility change, also from a restored setup (the layer panel's eyes follow).
- Its share of the setup (brief function 20, ADR-0031 point 5; `ctx.setup`): the projection, the background, the centre in lon/lat, the zoom as metres per pixel at the centre, the dim level, and the layers whose local visibility differs from their own `visible` field (at most 500), as JSON with a version. It tells the shell after every move and every change of map type, dim or visibility (the shell saves at most about once per second). Restoring (at start, and when the user picks a named setup) applies all of it: a layer that no longer exists is ignored, a layer the share does not name gets its own field again; until the map moves, the share gives the centre and zoom exactly as they were read, also a centre beyond what the projection shows, so an unchanged setup is not saved again (ITA, owner, 2026-10-19); a share that comes before the first snapshot keeps its layers until the snapshot, which forgets those that are gone. A background whose plugin has not started yet is waited for: it is shown once its provider comes, and only if it is still missing 10 s later does a notice say so (owner, 2026-10-17); meanwhile the share keeps the one the user had, until the user chooses another background or projection. The wait also applies to a named setup (the map view cannot tell it from the start). Layers the map does not know yet (before the first snapshot, or named only by objects) keep their saved visibility in every save. `setMapType` and a restore are heard once by `onMapTypeChange`, with the result. A share it cannot read changes nothing and shows in the plugin status.
- Workspaces (brief functions 22 to 25, ADR-0039, SDK 1.2.0; T-085, owner's answers of 2026-10-28): one map for the workspace the page shows (`ctx.workspace`); its controls and its three actions are in every workspace (`workspaces: ['*']`). A switch:
  - **the moment:** the shell sends `emc2:workspace-switch` (`detail.workspace`) on the element it gave `mount`, before the fresh snapshots and before any `onChange` listener (an agreement with the shell, not an SDK member). The map view then resolves a running interaction with `null`, clears every fade list and the selection, remembers the active layer of the workspace left (only a layer of that workspace, and not while a switch is already under way), and applies the new workspace's local visibility, telling `onLayerVisibilityChange` of each layer that changed. With no Map Pane mounted, these steps come with `onChange`;
  - **its `onChange` listener** makes the workspace's remembered active layer active again, if the workspace's snapshot still has it (when the snapshot comes later, it checks then). With nothing remembered (a first visit) it sets none: an active layer of another workspace becomes null, while one of this workspace that layer-panel made active from the fresh snapshot stays (layer-panel makes the top layer active). A layer made active between the moment and this listener is not remembered;
  - **from the moment until `onChange`** no layer is drawn, picked or clustered, while the eyes stay as they are. The shell restores the workspace's share just before `onChange`, so the objects of the fresh snapshot are first drawn at the workspace's own centre and zoom, never at the old one's (ITA finding e, owner, 2026-10-28). After a reload this can take up to 2 s, since the server gives a subscription at most one snapshot every 2 s and the shell waits for every fresh snapshot; meanwhile the map shows the old background without objects. Normally it takes a few milliseconds;
  - **until the fresh snapshot**, the old workspace's layers stay hidden (not drawn, picked, selected or highlighted), so only the new workspace's objects can be reached;
  - **the map state** is the workspace's share of the setup, which the shell restores; on a first visit nothing is restored, so the map keeps its type, centre, zoom and dim level, and the layers start with their `visible` field;
  - the live picture follows the workspace shown and draws its fresh snapshot from `onChange` on.
- View's layers as a reference (brief function 23, open questions 3 and 6): in a declared workspace View's layers are off until the user switches one on (`setLayerVisible`, layer-panel's reference group). Each workspace keeps its own local visibility during the page and in its own share (same format: the layers whose visibility differs from their default there, off for a reference). A reference layer is drawn under every layer of the workspace, in View's order among themselves (its symbols' texts and its shape labels, like all texts, lie on the one labels layer above all objects); its objects are not picked, selected by a click, Ctrl/Cmd+click or a box, or highlighted (even if another plugin puts their ids in the selection), and a right click on one opens the map actions. While one is on, the subscription names the workspace shown and View; otherwise it names none and follows the workspace shown. It is made again only when that changes, and from `onChange` after a switch. A layer the map has not seen during the page, named by no object, counts as a reference in a declared workspace until its snapshot shows otherwise; the eyes then follow.
- Fade lists (brief function 24, ADR-0040): `createFadeList()` gives a plugin its own list (`set` replaces it, `clear` empties it, at most 10 000 ids). The map view fades the union of all lists as it fades an expired object (once if both), at once, also an object that arrives later; a faded object is still picked and selected. A delete takes the id off every list; a switch clears every list at the moment. A list stays until its plugin clears it, also when the plugin stops, as an overlay does: nothing tells the map view that a plugin has stopped (brief function 24 and criterion 48, amended by the owner, 2026-10-28, T-085 Q3).
- `goTo` beyond what the projection can show (outside its world extent in lon/lat, or outside the extent in the projection's own map units, e.g. south of about 50° N on EPSG:3413's axes) centres on the nearest point it can show (owner, 2026-10-12). `viewport.center` gives the centre actually shown as soon as `goTo` returns, so a caller that reports where the map went should read it there instead of repeating the point it asked for; the map view logs an info line when it clamps.

Code: `web/src/core/` holds all decisions without OpenLayers (tested in jsdom); `web/src/engine/` is the only code that uses OpenLayers.

## Known limits

- The projection, background, view, dim level and local layer visibility are kept across page loads and browsers only through the user's setup (the shell's remembered and named setups); the background chosen per projection is also kept in the browser (`localStorage`), as before. Without a saved setup the map starts with the settings' initial view and each layer's `visible` field.
- **What a frame costs without a GPU** (T-195; the ITA's slow map with an area per dwell, 2026-11-14): with an area per dwell the map took 0.07 s to draw at 10 areas, 1.7 s at 22 and 7.7 s at 70 in a headless browser without a GPU (OpenLayers 10.10, software WebGL). Measured with the real simulator and gmti (53 areas, 53 detections, 53 pairing lines): hiding the dwell areas took a frame from 950 ms to 100 ms; the cost was in the WebGL **stroke rules**, not in the geometry or the fills: (1) every rule of the style is a pass over every vertex of the layer (the filter discards in the shader, so a rule costs the same however few features it matches) and a stroke rule costs about 50 ms a frame here, and the style had three per kind (solid, dashed, dotted) for shapes and for relation lines and speed vectors; now one rule for each, the dash an expression of the feature's own dash number (`strokeOf` in `engine/styles.ts`; a solid line has a dash too long to end and no gap, pixel for pixel as before); (2) a WebGL layer renders a second time for hit detection every frame, which nothing here uses (picking is the map view's own, from candidates), so the layers are made with `disableHitDetection`; (3) a polygon whose fill cannot be seen (gmti's areas have `fillOpacity` 0) is not filled (`P.fillOn`; a small gain, 15 % of a frame at 66 areas). Together a frame at 66 areas takes 190 ms instead of 740, a screenshot of the map at 42 areas 0.13 s instead of 1.5 s, and 300 large areas 1.1 s instead of 4.2 s. **What is left is per vertex:** about 13 µs a vertex a frame without a GPU (an area of 40° from 27 to 77 km has 180 to 250 vertices: `ARC_TOLERANCE` is 0.5 m, so an arc has a point every 0.4°), so 1 000 such areas draw in about 3.5 s a frame without a GPU (300 take 1.1 s); with a GPU the same is a few milliseconds. The fixes are fewer vertices (a looser `ARC_TOLERANCE`: 2 m instead of 0.5 m halves them and shows no chord at 2 m a pixel or coarser; at the highest zoom, 1 cm a pixel, an arc would show its chords) or fewer areas (see the handover).
- Texts of symbols and shape labels are drawn on one Canvas layer above all WebGL layers, so a lower layer's text can lie over a higher layer's object (only below the text threshold; owner, 2026-10-22: labels on top, not a labels layer per map layer). When the symbol atlas is full (a marker is drawn instead), the full image with text is drawn there, above every layer.
- WMS backgrounds (T-072) ask for `tileSize` pixels a tile, or the view's size in CSS pixels, whatever the screen's pixel ratio (the SDK's contract names the sizes), so on a high-density screen they are drawn scaled up. Tiles stay one grid per projection (22 levels from the extent), not the server's own grid; the browser caches the images, the map view keeps no cache (ADR-0035 point 7).
- Workspaces (T-085):
  - **mid-switch with a reference layer on** (owner, 2026-10-28, Q2): such a subscription gets no fresh snapshot, so from the moment until it is made again at `onChange` (the shell waits up to 5 s for the others' snapshots) the map draws nothing, rather than the old workspace's objects.
  - a switch whose last steps never come for another reason than a newer switch (e.g. the shell's restore fails) leaves the map as the moment left it until the next switch: nothing drawn, and with a reference layer on the subscription not made again (review F4).
  - other plugins' overlays (`createOverlay`) are not layers and are not held mid-switch: one that draws at map positions shows at the old workspace's centre until the shell restores the new one's share (normally milliseconds, up to 2 s after a reload; brief function 22 holds only the layers). An `edit` asked for mid-switch rejects with `NotOnMapError`, as for an object the map does not draw.
  - the fade list bound of 10 000 ids is the map view's own; neither the SDK nor ADR-0040 states one.
  - criterion 49 (1 000 tracks at 1 Hz in Simulation) is the ITA's (owner, 2026-10-28, Q4).
- Without the symbology plugin, symbols are drawn as grey markers (status `DEGRADED`).
- Symbol images are not drawn for high-DPI screens (device pixel ratio > 1 looks soft).
- In EPSG:3857 the map repeats sideways; in a projection that does not (EPSG:3413) the view's centre stays inside the projection's extent.
- The highlight frame fits the symbol's image without text amplifiers or the direction arrow: text drawn around a symbol (below the text threshold) and a long arrow can reach outside the frame. The highlight is drawn above all map layers, so the casing of a selected line or outline covers objects of higher layers next to it. At most 1 000 selected objects are highlighted.
- Pick uses OpenLayers' spatial index, not the WebGL hit buffer: symbols are hit by their image box, not their exact outline.
- **Right click while placing and drawing** (T-102's last part, brief function 27, criteria 52 and 53; owner, 2026-10-30; SDK 1.3.0 part 4): no menu then, and `pickPoint` ends with `null` as Escape does (back to selection); in a polygon or a line (a route) a right click takes back the last point set and the preview follows, and with no point left it acts as Escape, the drawing returns `null`; in a circle, ellipse, square or rectangle it acts as Escape before the press and does nothing during the press and drag; in an Edit and in a grabbed `dragObject` it does nothing; with nothing running, or while a `dragObject` only waits, the menu opens as before. While a shape is drawn the engine keeps the right button's press and release away from OpenLayers' Draw (it would take them for a point or the start of a drag). On a Mac, Ctrl+click counts as a right click, as for the menu. The fake map view's `rightClick()` is the other plugins' test of it.
- **Tactical graphics** (M5, brief functions 29 to 32, criteria 61 to 70; SDK 1.4.0, ADR-0043; T-144): a `core.multipoint` object with a symbol is drawn through the symbology service's `renderGraphic`, not as a marker.
  - **When it asks** (`core/graphics.ts`, `GraphicRenderer`): for each graphic whose control points are in the view (the view's extent one and a half times larger), with the control points (a `Polygon`'s ring without its closing position), the view's `resolution` in metres per pixel at the centre and the extent one and a half times the view's, in longitude, latitude (west greater than east across the antimeridian). Again after a zoom (a resolution that differs by more than 0.5 %, so a pan in Web Mercator is no zoom), when the view leaves the extent drawn, and when the object changes (its standard, SIDC, amplifiers or points). An answer for an older state of the object is dropped, and what is still wanted is asked for again. Up to 200 requests are in flight. The answers go to the engine in chunks, as many as fit in half of a 20 ms slice at the rate of the last chunk, so that no task on the main thread takes long (spike S1).
  - **Until it answers, without a service or its `renderGraphic`, and after a refusal** the graphic is the fallback marker of function 4 at its first point. The reason is logged once per object and state (a changed object logs again). An APP-6B graphic and a `vessel` symbol (SDK 1.4.0 part 3) are never asked for: they keep the marker and say so (`drawsGraphics`: APP-6 D and E only; vessels are drawn later, T-139).
  - **Drawn** (`engine/graphicFeatures.ts`): one WebGL feature per line and per fill, with the colours, opacities, widths and dashes as the service gives them, in every look; no halo (owner, Q2, 2026-11-06: `GraphicLabel.halo` is ignored). The style draws them after the shapes and before the markers, arrows and symbols, so within a layer: shapes, graphics, symbols; the layers keep their order. A dash pattern is drawn whole, in WebGL too (owner, 2026-11-06: the weather graphics have dash, gap, dot, gap and longer): an odd count is repeated once as the Canvas does, up to 16 numbers; the style has a rule for each different pattern (up to 24, then solid), the feature carries only the pattern's number, and the style is made again when a new pattern comes (a WebGL line program has about seven attributes to spare, so the numbers cannot be properties of the feature). Points that come back wrapped across the antimeridian (179.5° then −179.5°) are taken the short way, in lines and in fill rings; a hatched fill is the same hatch tile as a shape's, in the fill's colour and opacity. Labels are Canvas texts in the service's font, colour, angle (degrees clockwise) and alignment, above the layers; a graphic with labels counts once toward the text threshold, as a named shape does. In EPSG:3857 a line that jumps across the antimeridian is unwrapped and goes on into the next copy of the world; the polar projections draw it as it is. A faded object and a fade list fade all its parts.
  - **Selected, dragged** (functions 30, 31): a click, a box, a right click and `pick` hit a graphic on its lines (the pick tolerance), a fill only on its outline, the marker as the marker; the highlight draws its lines again with the selection's wider stroke on a dark casing (the outlines of its fills too). A drag shows the drawn graphic moved by the difference in longitude and latitude, without drawing it again, and gives the first control point moved by the drag as the drop position.
  - **Placed, edited** (function 32): `draw('line' | 'polygon', { graphic })` previews the graphic itself, drawn from the points set so far with the pointer as the next point, one request at a time and the newest points next; the plain line shows while the points (the pointer counted) are fewer than the graphic's `minPoints` and after a refusal. Enter and the double-click do not finish before `minPoints` points are set; it finishes by itself at `maxPoints` (both from `graphicCatalog`, default 2 and none until it answers). The result is the geometry without a graphic, a polygon's ring in the clicked order with its closing position last. `edit` of a `core.multipoint` shows a handle on each control point, the graphic drawn again as a point (or the whole graphic) is dragged, and resolves with the geometry type the object has, also a `MultiPoint` (one point is edited as a point).
  - **Limits:** a graphic is asked for only when one of its control points is in the view or near it (a large graphic whose points are all off screen does not draw until one comes near); the graphics that cross themselves in a fill are not cut (T-111 is for shapes); the fill of a graphic is picked on its outline only, never inside (function 30). Up to 200 requests are in flight (the symbology service batches the requests of a frame).
  - **Images inside graphics** (M6, brief function 37, criteria 89 to 93; SDK 1.5.0 part 2, CR-0039): the service's `images` (the icons of minefields, the mine line and the contaminated areas) are drawn centred on their position, `width` by `height` screen pixels, turned `angle` degrees clockwise on the screen, after the graphic's lines and under its labels, faded with it, never picked, and in the preview. Only an SVG data URI is the source of an image (`core/graphicImages.ts`; anything else, or a number that cannot be drawn, is left out and nothing is requested); each `svg` text is decoded once on the main thread and cached (`engine/graphicImages.ts`), and the graphic draws without it while it decodes. They are drawn with the labels, so they follow the text threshold and lie over the symbols of their layer. Without `images` a graphic draws as in M5.
- **An area is picked by its outline only** (T-102's first half, brief function 28, criterion 54; owner, 2026-10-30): inside a polygon, circle, ellipse, square or rectangle, filled or not, a click, a Ctrl/Cmd+click, a right click, `pick` and the press that grabs a selected shape pass through to what lies under it (a symbol inside a shape is always picked), or hit empty map. The outline counts within its width and the pick tolerance. The selection box is unchanged. The brief's function 28 says shapes without a fill are picked as before; the owner decided otherwise (to be written into the brief).
- **A polygon whose outline crosses itself** (T-111; owner, 2026-10-31: every area the outline encloses is filled, the middle of a star too, the nonzero rule): the WebGL fill cuts a ring into triangles as if it were simple, which a bow tie or a star is not. The map view cuts such a ring at its crossings into simple faces (`core/crossing.ts`), draws them as a fill feature of its own under the polygon's (`<id>#fill`, no outline, not picked), and the polygon's own feature keeps the outline and has no fill. Convex rings, the vast majority, are not looked at again after one pass; a non-convex simple ring of 64 points takes about 0.014 ms. **With holes** (M8): the outer ring's faces are cut as above and each hole is cut out of the face that holds it (`fillFacesWithHoles`; before, such a polygon was drawn as it came and one lobe of a bow tie with a hole stayed empty). Limits: a hole that lies across the boundary of a face (over a crossing of the outer ring) cannot be cut out of one face without clipping and is left out, so that part is filled; a hole that crosses itself is not looked at; faces with a crossing at exactly one point of three edges are not cut right; the fill of a tactical graphic is not cut (shapes only). Drawing a bow tie whose two lobes cancel (signed area of nothing) is no longer refused as "no area".
- Menus and own actions (T-054 part E, browser check 2026-10-17): until the CDA's part 8 the shell's `ctx.actions` does nothing (it logs that once), so the menu is not shown yet and the actions are not offered; the map view's side was checked in the browser by recording what it asks the shell to open (a selection of 3, a position on empty map), and Centre on and Zoom to through `MapView`. Zoom to fits the whole map area: the Map Pane's own controls and notices (top right) can cover an object right at the margin.
- Selection (T-054 part D, browser check 2026-10-16; rechecked with `draw-symbols`' steps 2 and 3, 2026-10-20): `draw-symbols` no longer handles clicks or Escape for the selection; it keeps a `dragObject` waiting for the selected symbol. The map view itself was checked by tracing every `selection.set`. The Mac keys are tested with a simulated platform only (no Mac here). A box over 1 500 held objects takes 2–14 ms. Each box with something left out gives its own notice. Objects outside the subscribed area are not counted in the notice (the map does not know them).
- Texts of symbols (T-087, browser check 2026-10-22, dev VM without a GPU): a symbol's text image is its image with text with the symbol cut out, about 0.35 ms each to build; with 2 000 symbols with text newly in view one refresh takes about 0.78 s (before T-087 about 0.6 s the first time on a page and 0.05 s once the symbol images were cached). The images are kept per object, so a refresh of symbols already seen takes about 20 ms, as before. Accepted by the owner, 2026-10-22.
- Performance and real pixels are checked by the ITA's browser tests (criteria 8, 9). A manual check on the dev VM (no GPU): 10 000 symbols with direction arrows drew and panned with frame gaps of about 60–95 ms. 10 000 hatched squares (criterion 39, T-054 prototype, 2026-10-16), timed per frame with the rasterising forced to finish: with all in view a median of 53–56 ms and at most 80 ms during a pan, the same as solid or outline-only areas; the frame after a pan, which rebuilds the buffers, about 100 ms; zoomed in about 7 ms. 10 000 shapes with all the 1.1.0 styles (T-054 part B, 2026-10-16; a quarter each hatched named squares, hatched named ellipses, named lines with arrowheads, named routes with markers; 15 000 features): with all in view a median of 171–186 ms and at most 289–360 ms during a pan, the settle frame 259–430 ms, under criterion 8's 0.5 s but with a smaller margin. The cost follows the areas' vertices (the ellipses' 65-point rings, 162 500 of the 175 000), not the hatch, arrowheads or markers (the same with those rules off). At 1 500 m/px (1 344 labels drawn) a median of 65 ms; zoomed in about 8 ms. Fewer ring points for small circles and ellipses would give more margin (not done).
- Geodesic edges are great circles on a sphere (mean earth radius), not exact WGS84 ellipsoid geodesics.
- The direction-of-movement arrow is drawn by the map view, not by the symbology plugin, so that one symbol image serves every heading. It starts at the symbol's anchor and is drawn under the symbol, so the shaft does not cross the image (T-138, owner, 2026-11-05; the WebGL rule of the arrow comes before the symbol rules, and the Canvas styles of overlays and previews already had it first); APP-6 starts it at the end of a staff below the frame for land symbols. Fixing that needs the SymbolService to tell where the arrow starts (a possible future SDK addition).
- Clusters: `byLayer` and `byIdentity` are separate totals, so with a layer hidden locally the count is exact but the identity split is scaled in proportion (owner, 2026-10-11).
- A vector background that fails to load stays chosen, with "Background unavailable" and the graticule; the map does not switch to another background by itself. Preparing a vector background takes time once per projection: with the real Natural Earth 1:50m data (165 730 points) about 190–210 ms in EPSG:3857, 130–140 ms in EPSG:3413 and 55–70 ms in EPSG:3031, plus about 50 ms to parse the file (measured in Node on the dev VM).
- The pieces of a polygon cut at ±180° meet along the same meridian but stay separate polygons, and Natural Earth's pieces do not share their points there; a fill-only opaque layer gets an outline in its fill colour to hide the line where they meet. With a translucent fill the line may show.
- A faded object's highlight is faded alike (brief function 13), so the frame of a selected expired object is faint.
- Fading compares expiry with this browser's clock: a browser whose clock is off fades objects early or late.
- A line with both an arrowhead and route markers gets a marker on its last point too, over the arrowhead's tip.
- While a shape is edited, the object itself stays drawn where it was until the caller sends the change; the edited copy is drawn on top. Reshaping a square or rectangle does not keep its right angles (ADR-0033, `draw-shapes` brief).
- An arrowhead turns along the last segment that has a length, so a line ending with a repeated point still points the right way.
- An object with no position inside the current projection's valid area (its `worldExtent`) is not drawn and not pickable in that projection, e.g. an Arctic object in EPSG:3031 (owner, 2026-10-12); it stays in the live picture and the selection and is drawn again in a projection where it lies. An object partly inside is drawn whole. The same holds for a cluster, by its position (owner, 2026-10-12): zoomed out in EPSG:3413, the area the shell asks for follows the screen and reaches far south of 30° N, and those clusters are not drawn or clicked; the server still counts their objects against the budget.
