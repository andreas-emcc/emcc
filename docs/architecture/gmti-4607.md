# GMTI: STANAG 4607 (AEDP-4607 Edition A) and how emc2 uses it

A reference for the agents that build, test or feed `gmti` (T-149): the PDA (the `gmti` brief), PCA-gmti, PCA-simulator (the GMTI sensor), the ESA (the playback) and the ITA. It explains the format in the ACA's own words and maps it to emc2 as ADR-0048 decides. It replaces no source: when it and the standard differ, the standard wins, and the agent tells the ACA.

**Sources** (owner, Q2 of the M5 plan): STANAG 4607 Edition 4 (16 February 2024) is the agreement to use **AEDP-4607 Edition A, Version 1** (the format) with **AEDP-4607.1 Edition A, Version 1** (the implementation guide). All three are public NSDD documents in a local folder of the owner's. Agents read them there; they are never copied into the repository. Citations below are "AEDP 3.4.6" (the format, section 3.4.6), "AEDP Table 3-9", "AEDP Annex C-4.6", and "Guide D.3", "Guide Table C-1" (the implementation guide).

**Field ids** are the standard's: `P` packet header, `S` segment header, `M` mission, `D` dwell (`D32.n` a target report), `H` HRR, `J` job definition, `F` free text, `T` test and status, `C` processing history, `L` platform location, `R` job request, `A` job acknowledge. `M`/`C`/`O` after a field means mandatory, conditional or optional (AEDP 2.4).

## 0. The concepts in one page

- A **GMTI radar** reports moving things on the surface: land, littoral and sea, plus stationary rotators and low, slow flyers (AEDP 1.1).
- A **job** is one tasking of the radar: one radar mode, one **bounding area** (a convex quadrilateral), one job id (AEDP 3.7, Annex C-1.5). A job's **revisits** are its repeated scans of that area; each revisit is made of **dwells**, one look of the beam at a part of the area (Annex C-1.3, C-1.4).
- A **dwell segment** is the format's unit of reporting: one time, one sensor position, one **dwell area** on the ground, and zero or more **target reports** (AEDP 3.4). It need not be one physical radar dwell (Annex C-1.3; Guide D.2). A dwell segment is sent even when nothing was detected.
- A **target report** ("a dot") is one detection: a position, usually a radial speed, optionally a class and the measurement errors (AEDP 3.4.32). Reports are not tracks; associating them is a tracker's job. This matches ADR-0048 point 3.
- A **packet** is a packet header plus any number of segments, each with its own 5-byte segment header. A packet carries data of one job (AEDP 2.1).
- The **platform** carries the **sensor**; the format reports the sensor's position per dwell (`D7`–`D9`) and the platform's attitude (`D21`–`D23`), and, when not collecting, the platform's position in its own segment (AEDP 3.15). The guide treats sensor position and platform position as the same point (Guide E.8).
- **Time** is UTC milliseconds since midnight of the mission's **reference day**, which only the mission segment gives (AEDP 3.3, Annex C-3).
- **Positions** are WGS84 latitude and longitude as binary angles, heights in metres or centimetres above the WGS84 ellipsoid (Annex C-2). The format writes latitude first; emc2 writes longitude first (ADR-0007).

## 1. The protocol

### 1.1 Bytes and data types (AEDP 2.3, Annex C-4)

- **Big-endian** everywhere: the most significant byte first, bit 7 of a byte is its most significant bit (Annex C-4.1). Fields start on byte boundaries; there are no half bytes (AEDP 2.3).
- **No framing characters**, no checksum, no encryption: the format assumes the layer below delivers whole, error-free packets or nothing (AEDP 2.2; Guide D.3). Packets may be lost.
- **Alphanumeric fields** (`A`) are left-justified and padded with spaces (`0x20`); the allowed characters are the Basic Character Set, `0x20`–`0x7E` plus LF, FF and CR (Annex A). Treat a field of all spaces as "none".

| Type | Size | Meaning | Decode | Encode |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| `I8`, `I16`, `I32` | 1, 2, 4 | unsigned integer | the integer | the integer |
| `S8`, `S16`, `S32`, `S64` | 1, 2, 4, 8 | two's-complement signed integer | the integer | the integer |
| `E8` | 1 | enumeration | look up the value | the value |
| `FL8`, `FL16`, `FL40`, `FL64` | 1, 2, 5, 8 | flag bits, 1 = on | per bit | per bit |
| `BA16`, `BA32` | 2, 4 | **binary angle**, 0 ≤ angle < 360°, unsigned: the msb is 180°, the next 90°, and so on | `value × 360 / 2^n` | `round(angle × 2^n / 360)`, after `angle mod 360` |
| `SA16`, `SA32` | 2, 4 | **signed binary angle**, −90° ≤ angle < 90°, two's complement: the bit after the sign is 45° | `signed(value) × 180 / 2^n` | `round(angle × 2^n / 180)` as two's complement |
| `B16`, `B32` | 2, 4 | **signed binary decimal**, sign and magnitude: 1 sign bit, 8 integer bits, 7 or 23 fraction bits | `(−1)^s × (int + frac / 2^7 or 2^23)` | split; the magnitude's largest value is 256 − 1/128 (B16) |
| `H32` | 4 | HRR's "signed hertz decimal": 1 sign bit, 15 integer bits, 16 fraction bits | as B32 with 15/16 split | as B32 with 15/16 split |

Checks, from the standard's own examples (Annex C-4.6, C-4.7): `BA16 0x591C` = 125.31006°, and −34.876099° = `SA16 0xCE66`. The resolutions: BA16 0.0055°, SA16 0.0027°, BA32 8.4e−8° (about 1 cm at the equator), SA32 4.2e−8°. B16's smallest step is 1/128 (AEDP Annex C-4.5). The ranges printed in the tables, such as "0 to 359.9945" for a BA16, are the encodings' limits, not operational limits.

Longitude in this format is **0 to 360° east**. For emc2's −180..180 (ADR-0007): decode, then subtract 360 when the result is above 180; encode after adding 360 to a negative longitude. The signed types `S16` for the deltas and `S32` for the altitude are ordinary integers, not angles.

### 1.5 Dwell segment (AEDP 3.4, Table 3-9)

The dwell segment is variable: an 8-byte **existence mask** says which of the other fields are present, in the fixed order below; absent fields take no bytes (AEDP 3.4.1). Then come `D5` target reports, each with the same per-report mask bits. Sizes: the body is **43 bytes with only the mandatory fields**, 86 with every field; a report is 4 bytes in the smallest reduced form (the two deltas; 6 with the report index) and up to 32 with every field (one position form at a time).

**Existence mask layout** (AEDP Figure 3-1). Byte 7 is transmitted first; bit 7 is its msb. The mask's first bit stands for `D2`, and each next bit for the next field; the report fields follow the body fields; bytes 1 and 0 are spare and zero.

| Byte | bit 7 | 6 | 5 | 4 | 3 | 2 | 1 | 0 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 7 | D2 | D3 | D4 | D5 | D6 | D7 | D8 | D9 |
| 6 | D10 | D11 | D12 | D13 | D14 | D15 | D16 | D17 |
| 5 | D18 | D19 | D20 | D21 | D22 | D23 | D24 | D25 |
| 4 | D26 | D27 | D28 | D29 | D30 | D31 | D32.1 | D32.2 |
| 3 | D32.3 | D32.4 | D32.5 | D32.6 | D32.7 | D32.8 | D32.9 | D32.10 |
| 2 | D32.11 | D32.12 | D32.13 | D32.14 | D32.15 | D32.16 | D32.17 | D32.18 |
| 1, 0 | spare, all 0 | | | | | | | |

So a segment with the mandatory fields only has the mask `0xFF 00 03 C0 00 00 00 00`. The guide's situation-awareness example, `0xFF C7 1F C2 60 00 00 00`, adds the scale factors, sensor track and speed, platform orientation and the delta positions (Guide Table C-1). **Rule for an empty dwell:** when `D5` is 0, no report fields follow, whatever the report bits say; producers may keep a constant mask (AEDP 3.4.1).

**The dwell area** (Annex C-1.12, Figure C-4) is the ground patch the beam covered: centred at `D24`/`D25`, extending `D26` km towards and away from the sensor along the ground, and `D27` degrees either side of the line from the sensor to the centre. It is a sector of an annulus around the sensor, which is emc2's `ArcBand` (section 4.2).

**Two position forms.** Full precision uses 4 + 4 bytes per report. The reduced form sends 2 + 2 bytes per report plus the two scale factors once per dwell; the sender chooses scale factors so that one step of the delta is below the sensor's resolution, splitting the dwell when it cannot (Guide E.7). A receiver recovers the position with the formulas above; the guide shows how to do it in 32-bit integer arithmetic and warns about longitudes on either side of the prime meridian (Guide E.7). In floating point: decode `D24`, `D25`, `D10`, `D11` to degrees first, then `lat = D24° + D32.4 × D10°` and `lon = D25° + D32.5 × D11°`, then normalise the longitude.

Reports of one dwell may be **split over several dwell segments** with the same indices, each carrying its own `D5` and repeating the body; different radar modes or processors go in different segments (AEDP 3.4.32; Guide D.2, D.5).

### 1.6 HRR segment (AEDP 3.5, Tables 3-12, 3-13)

High range resolution data: a range-Doppler "chip" or profile of one target, or a whole range-Doppler map. It has its own 5-byte existence mask (`H1`, Figure 3-4: byte 4 bit 7 = `H2`, … byte 1 bits 1–0 = `H32.1`, `H32.2`, byte 0 bits 7–6 = `H32.3`, `H32.4`), the same revisit, dwell and last-dwell fields as the dwell (`H2`–`H4`), and `H5`, the MTI report index it belongs to (conditional: absent for a map that matches no single report). The rest describes the chip: counts of range and Doppler samples (`H6`–`H8`), powers in quarter decibels (`H9`, `H10`, `H19`), resolutions and spacings in cm and Hz as `B16` or `H32` (`H11`–`H14`), centre frequency in GHz (`H15`, B32), compression and weighting enumerations (`H16`–`H18`), the chip type (`H23`: 1 = 1-D profile, 2 = 2-D chip, 3 = sparse chip, 4 = oversized chip, 5 = full map, 6 = partial map, 7 = range-pulse data), a processing mask (`H24`), the bytes per scatterer magnitude and phase (`H25` 1 or 2, `H26` 0, 1 or 2), and optional chip geometry (`H21`, `H22`, `H27`–`H31`). Then the **scatterer records** (`H32`): magnitude in −dB/4 (1 or 2 bytes), optional phase (1 or 2 bytes, 2π/256 or 2π/65536 steps), and for sparse chips a range and Doppler index (2 + 2 bytes), ordered by range then Doppler (AEDP 3.5.32).

emc2 does not use HRR in M5. A receiver needs only `S2` to skip it.

### 1.8 Free text segment (AEDP 3.8, Table 3-19)

`F1` originator id (A, 10), `F2` recipient id (A, 10), `F3` the text (A, 1 to 65 515 bytes, BCS). The ids have no formal meaning (AEDP 3.8 note). Sent as required; meant for annotations, not for new data (Guide F, question 15).

### 1.9 Test and status segment, 14 bytes (AEDP 3.12, Table 3-20)

`T1` job id (I32), `T2` revisit index (I16), `T3` dwell index (I16), `T4` dwell time (I32, ms as D6), `T5` hardware status (FL8: bit 7 antenna, 6 RF electronics, 5 processor, 4 datalink, 3 calibration mode; 1 = failed), `T6` mode status (FL8: bit 7 range, 6 azimuth, 5 elevation, 4 temperature limit exceeded). Sent as required or when a job request asked for it.

### 1.10 Processing history segment (AEDP 3.14, Tables 3-21 to 3-23)

Says that a system changed the data after the sensor produced it. Body: `C1` record count (I8), `C2`–`C5` the **original** job's nationality (A 2), platform id (A 10), mission id (I32) and job id (I32). Then `C1` records of 23 bytes: `C6.1` sequence (I8), `C6.2`–`C6.5` the modifying system's nationality, platform, mission and job ids, `C6.6` processing performed (FL16, Table 3-23: area filtering 0x0001, classification filtering 0x0002, velocity filtering 0x0004, SNR filtering 0x0008, de-clutter 0x0010, bandwidth filtering 0x0020, revisit filtering 0x0040, location adjustment 0x0080, geoid adjustment 0x0100, location registration 0x0200, time filtering 0x0400, security filtering 0x0800, data augmentation 0x1000, coordinate conversion 0x2000). Each system that processes appends a record and sends the segment every 3 minutes (AEDP 3.14). Not sent for unprocessed data (Guide F, question 11).

### 1.11 Platform location segment, 23 bytes (AEDP 3.15, Table 3-24)

For periods when the sensor is not collecting: `L1` time (I32, ms as D6), `L2` latitude (SA32), `L3` longitude (BA32), `L4` altitude (S32, cm above the ellipsoid), `L5` track (BA16, degrees), `L6` speed (I32, mm/s), `L7` vertical velocity (S8, dm/s). All mandatory.

### 1.12 Job request and job acknowledge (AEDP chapter 4)

Recommended, not required. **Job request** (type 101, 79 bytes): requestor id and task id (A 10 each), priority (I8, 0 default, 1–99), bounding area (as J6–J13), radar mode (E8), range and cross-range resolution (I16 cm, I16 dm), earliest start as year, month, day, hour, minute, second (I16, I8 ×5; second may be 60), allowed delay (I16 s), duration (I16 s, 0 = continuous), revisit interval (I16 ds, 0 = default), sensor type (E8) and model (A 6), request type (FL8: 0 new, 1 cancel). **Job acknowledge** (type 102, 79 bytes): the job id given, the requestor id and task id, sensor type and model, radar priority, the bounding area, radar mode, duration, revisit interval, the **request status** (E8: 0 request, 1 approved, 2 approved with modification, 3 denied line of sight, 4 timeline, 5 orbit, 6 priority, 7 area of interest, 8 illegal request, 9 function inoperative, 10 other), the start time (year … second) and the requestor's nationality (A 2). emc2 does not task sensors in M5.

## 3. The rules

### 3.1 Order and nesting

- Every packet starts with the packet header; every segment with its segment header; segments follow each other with no gaps and no padding (AEDP 2.1, 2.2). Any mix of segment types may share a packet, but all dwell and HRR segments in it belong to the job in `P10` (AEDP 3.1.10).
- A dwell segment is sent only in a packet whose `P10` is not 0 (AEDP 3.4). A packet whose `P10` is 0 carries only job-independent segments: mission, free text, status, history, platform location.
- The **job definition precedes the first dwell of its job** (AEDP 3.7; Guide G.9.5). After that, the mission segment repeats at least every 2 minutes, the job definition every 30 seconds, the processing history every 3 minutes (AEDP 3.3, 3.7, 3.14; Guide A.1.3).
- There is no nesting beyond "reports inside a dwell" and "scatterers inside an HRR segment". An HRR segment refers to a report by job, revisit, dwell and MTI report index (AEDP 3.5.5).
- Nothing says how many dwells a packet holds. A typical packet is one dwell (Guide Figure A-1); a minimal implementation may send and need only accept single-segment packets of at most 65 535 bytes (Guide G.7.3).

### 3.2 Revisit and dwell indices, the last-dwell flag

- `D2` counts revisits of the current job definition's bounding area, from 0; it resets to 0 when the sensor stops revisiting that area (AEDP 3.4.2). `D3` counts dwells within a revisit, from 0, and may wrap (AEDP 3.4.3). `D4` = 1 closes the revisit (AEDP 3.4.4). Annex C's Table C-1 walks through interleaved jobs and "windshield-wiper" scans.
- A sensor that has no dwell structure sends every look as `D3` = 0, `D4` = 1, and counts revisits with `D2` (AEDP 3.4.4 note). **This is what the simulator's sensor does.**
- The indices are a bookkeeping scheme that each sensor uses as suits it; the guide warns that two exploiters can reconstruct them differently (Annex C-1.6; Guide F, question 10). Use `(P8, P10, D2, D3)` to recognise a dwell and `D32.1` to recognise a report, and nothing more.
- Several dwell segments with the same indices are parts of one dwell (Guide D.2).

### 3.3 Time

- The clock is UTC (Annex C-3). A time is `D6` (or `T4`, `L1`) milliseconds after midnight of the reference day `M5`–`M7`, including whole days: the standard's example puts 08:45:35.2 on the day after the reference day at 117 935 200 (Annex C-3). The field holds about 49.7 days.
- **The reference day is state.** A receiver keeps the latest mission segment per platform and mission (`P8`, `P9`) and cannot place dwells on the calendar before it has one. A dwell received first is held or dropped; the link's counters say which (section 4.1).
- `D6` is the dwell's temporal centre. All reports in the dwell share it; a report has no time of its own (Guide F, question 6).

### 3.4 Coordinates and heights

- WGS84 geodetic latitude, longitude and height (Annex C-2): longitude 0–360° east, latitude ±90°, as the binary angles of section 1.1. **emc2 stores longitude first and in −180..180** (ADR-0007).
- **Heights:** the sensor's and platform's altitude (`D9`, `L4`) is centimetres above the ellipsoid; a target's height (`D32.6`) is metres above the ellipsoid. When `D32.6` is absent, the target sits on the earth model named in `J27`/`J28`: a terrain model over a geoid, or a flat earth (AEDP 3.4.32.6). Ellipsoid and geoid differ by tens of metres; the model the sensor used is in the job definition, and a receiver that shows heights says which reference it shows. The guide's E.8 allows heights from mean sea level where a geoid is used; the format's own fields are ellipsoidal.
- The position of a target comes from the sensor's range, azimuth and elevation and the earth model; the converter on the platform did that (Annex C-2; processing code 0x2000). emc2 never re-derives positions.
- Angles: tracks and headings clockwise from true north; platform heading, pitch, roll in that order of rotation (AEDP 3.4.21–3.4.23, Annex C-1.14); sensor heading, pitch, roll likewise, relative to the platform (Annex C-1.15).

### 3.5 The sensor's errors

Three layers, from coarse to fine: the job definition's **nominal** values (`J16`–`J26`), the dwell's **sensor position** errors (`D12`–`D14`, `D18`–`D20`), and the report's **measurement** errors (`D32.12`–`D32.15`), which the sensor sends only when it also sends the dwell's position errors (AEDP 3.4.32.12). All are one standard deviation. A report's position error is given along the radar's axes, slant range (cm) and cross range (dm), not as north and east errors; a tracker rotates them by the bearing from the sensor. The minimum detectable velocity (`D31`, `J24`) says which slow movers the sensor misses; the detection probability (`J25`) and false alarm density (`J26`) describe the whole job.

### 3.6 Unknown and unsupported content

- **Unknown segment type** (reserved, extension, or one the receiver does not implement): skip `S2` bytes and go on; count it per link (AEDP 3.2.1; Guide G.9.18 requires at least a warning and the option to continue).
- **Unknown enumeration value:** keep the number, show "unknown (n)". Enumerations grow between editions.
- **Set mask bits for reserved fields** (bytes 1 and 0 of `D1`): no bytes follow for them; ignore the bits.
- **A malformed packet** (sizes that do not add up, a segment past the packet's end, a mask that cannot be true): drop the whole packet, count it, keep the link up (Guide G.9.18). On a TCP stream, resynchronising after a bad size is guesswork; the practical rule is to close and reopen the connection, which the connection task's retry does (ADR-0036).
- **Older version ids** ("20", "21", "30"): the headers and the defined segments kept their layout; the differences are added enumeration values and `D32.18`. Parse them as Edition A; log a version other than "30" and "41" (section 4.1). The owner's recordings are all "21" or "30" (section 5.3).
- **Conditional rules not honoured.** Real senders set report error fields (`D32.12`–`D32.15`) without the dwell's `D12`–`D14`, or a velocity error without the velocity (section 5.3). The mask is the truth: read what it says is there, and treat the standard's conditions as advice for sending, not as checks when receiving.

### 3.7 What is mandatory for a minimal sender and receiver

The guide's **minimum implementation capability** (Guide G.7.3): packets of at most 65 535 bytes with one segment each, and the packet header, segment header, mission, dwell and job definition segments. For the dwell, the generator populates the mandatory fields `D1`–`D9` and `D24`–`D27`, sends a dwell even without targets, keeps one radar mode per segment, and supports at least 120 reports per dwell (Guide G.9.5). The guide's **exploitation classes** (Guide Annex C) list what a display and a tracker need beyond that: situation awareness adds the positions (reduced form), the sensor's track and speed and the platform's attitude; targeting and tracking adds the full-precision positions, height, radial and wrap velocity, SNR, classification and probability, and the sensor orientation.

**emc2's receiver** (section 4.1) meets the MIC and accepts multi-segment packets and packets up to a per-link cap. **emc2's sender** (section 4.3) sends what the targeting and tracking class needs, less what the simulator does not have.

## 4. emc2's mapping (ADR-0048)

ADR-0048 decides what `gmti` does; this section says which bytes that means. Where the brief must still choose, it says "brief".

### 4.1 `gmti` in: a link reads a stream of packets

An in link (TCP client, TCP server or UDP; ADR-0048 point 2, ADR-0036) parses packets and writes into its target layer through the object writer (SDK 1.4.0 part 1). Per link the admin sets the default label, the platform's symbol and identity, the cap of reports per dwell and the detection's expiry and fading defaults (ADR-0048 point 3).

**Per packet**

- `P1`: every version is read by the existence mask (section 3.6); a version other than "30" and "41" is logged once per link. The name of class `D32.10` 143 follows the packet's edition: the tagging device in Edition 3 and older, reserved in Edition A (M9.1).
- `P4`, `P5`, `P6`: the packet's label (ADR-0020). `P4` 1–5 becomes the label's classification in the link's policy (the admin maps the five values to the policy's names; the default policy is NATO's and `P5` "XN" matches it); the link's default label applies when the packet's classification cannot be mapped. `P6` is kept as a number in the extension; mapping code words to label categories is after M5. The brief says how a packet whose classification is above the link's layer is handled, as the output rule in ADR-0037 point 8 does for sending.
- `P7`: the APP-6 context of the objects the packet produces: 0 → reality, 1 and 2 → simulation, 128–130 → exercise.
- `P8`, `P9`, `P10`: the **platform key** is `P8` (per link); the **job key** is `(P8, P10)`. The dwell area shape is **one per platform**, as ADR-0048 says, with the job id as a field on it: one recording (section 5.3) starts a new job id with nearly every dwell, so a shape per job would churn, and the AFSERS streams run one job per platform anyway. Interleaved jobs on one platform then share the shape, which flickers between two areas; a per-job option is for later if a real stream needs it.

**Mission segment:** the reference day for `(P8, P9)`; `M3` the platform's type, shown in the extension and, until the admin sets a symbol, the basis of a default symbol (fixed wing for aircraft types, a ground sensor for 13–15, unknown for 0 and 255); `M1`, `M2`, `M4` text in the extension.

**Job definition:** kept per job key: sensor type and model, radar mode, bounding area, the nominal errors, the terrain and geoid model. They go in the `gmti` namespace of the platform object as the job's description (ADR-0007: only `gmti` writes `extensions.gmti`). A detection without its own errors gets the job's nominal ones. `J5` = 255 ends the job: the area shape is removed; the detections expire on their own.

**Dwell segment**

- The **platform object** (ADR-0048 point 3): a symbol at `D7`, `D8` (decoded, longitude first) with altitude `D9` / 100 m; heading from `D21` when present, else the track `D15`; speed `D16` / 1000 m/s; time `D6` on the reference day. Its id is stable per platform key (a UUID derived from the link id and `P8`); its symbol and identity are the link's settings; its name is `P8` trimmed.
- The **dwell area** (ADR-0048 point 3): one shape per key, replaced each dwell, as an `ArcBand` centred at the sensor position: `d` = geodesic distance from `(D8, D7)` to `(D25, D24)`, `b` = initial bearing between them (SDK geodesy, ADR-0019); `innerRadius = max(0, d − D26 × 1000)`, `outerRadius = d + D26 × 1000`, `startBearing = b − D27`, `endBearing = b + D27`, normalised to 0–360. When `D26` is 0 or `D27` is 0 the brief chooses a minimum size or hides the shape. The area's time is `D6`.
- **Target reports → `gmti.detection` objects** (ADR-0048 point 3), one each, in the link's target layer, with the packet's label:
  - position: `D32.2`/`D32.3` or the reduced form (section 1.5), as a `Point`, longitude first; height `D32.6` when present, else none;
  - time: `D6` on the reference day;
  - identity: unknown, context from `P7`; symbol: a ground track (the brief may map the classification to a few symbols: wheeled, tracked, rotary wing, fixed wing, maritime; the rest unknown);
  - `extensions.gmti`: `jobId`, `revisitIndex`, `dwellIndex`, `reportIndex` (`D32.1`), `radialVelocity` m/s (= `D32.7` / 100) and `wrapVelocity` m/s, `snr` dB, `classification` (the name, with the number) and `classificationProbability`, errors in metres and m/s (`slantRange` = `D32.12` / 100, `crossRange` = `D32.13` / 10, `height` = `D32.14`, `radialVelocity` = `D32.15` / 100), `truthTag` when present, `rcs` dB (= `D32.18` / 2), and the sensor's platform key;
  - expiry and fading from the data type's defaults (ADR-0023 point 7), so the detections make the short history that ADR-0048 wants and disappear by themselves;
  - **cap:** reports beyond the link's cap per dwell are skipped and counted (ADR-0048 point 3; ADR-0037 point 7). The cap counts over all segments of one dwell key.
- A dwell with `D5` = 0 updates the platform and the area only.

**Platform location segment:** updates the platform object's position, altitude, heading (`L5`) and speed (`L6`), time `L1`, with no area change.

**Skipped in M5:** HRR, free text (logged at debug), test and status (the brief may count `T5`/`T6` failures), processing history, job request and acknowledge, and all reserved and extension types. Each skip is counted per type in the link's counters, which the Monitor shows (ADR-0037 point 7).

**The link's counters** (ADR-0037 point 7): packets, bytes, dwells, reports written, reports skipped by the cap, segments skipped by type, packets dropped as malformed, dwells held or dropped for want of a mission segment, packets whose label could not be mapped.

### 4.2 Geometry: dwell area ↔ `ArcBand`

`Geometry.ArcBand(center, innerRadius, outerRadius, startBearing, endBearing)` is a band between two radii, clockwise from `startBearing` to `endBearing`, in metres and degrees. The format's dwell area is the same shape seen from the sensor. In: section 4.1. Out: from an `ArcBand` whose centre is the sensor, `D24`/`D25` = the point at bearing `(start + end) / 2` and distance `(inner + outer) / 2`, `D26` = `(outer − inner) / 2 / 1000` km, `D27` = `(end − start) / 2` degrees. An `ArcBand` whose centre is not the sensor is sent by its own shape in the same way, wherever its centre lies (T-193, `pca-gmti/m7` `120c06f6`): `D24`/`D25` its middle, `D26` half its depth, `D27` half its angle (the beam's half width, AEDP 3.4.27), and a cut (the limit below) is measured from the band's centre. From a `Circle` round the sensor (the simulator's "Detect all in range"; owner KM, 2026-11-07, CR-0035): `D24`/`D25` = the point half the radius out along any bearing, `D26` = half the radius, `D27` = 180°, which is the whole disc as 4607 allows (the VISTA recording's half disc is the same with 90°) and which 4.1's formula reads back as the same circle (inner 0, outer the radius). From an `Ellipse` or `Polygon`, or a `Circle` elsewhere (which `ChannelItem.SensorReport` also allows): `D24`/`D25` = the shape's centre or centroid, `D26` = half the shape's extent along the sensor-to-centre line, `D27` = half the angle the shape subtends at the sensor. The brief fixes the approximation; the test checks it on the simulator's own sector-shaped area, where it is exact. **The limit** (owner, JS, KA and KM, 2026-11-07; CR-0035; the `gmti` brief, G12): the sensor's reach is generic and not cut (the simulator's function 28), and `D26` holds at most 255.99 km, so `gmti` cuts: a larger range half extent is sent as 255.99 km **from the area's near edge**, the bearings unchanged. An `ArcBand` keeps its inner radius and reaches at most 511.98 km beyond it (its centre moves to 255.99 km past the inner edge); a `Circle` round the sensor, sent with its centre halfway out, goes whole up to a radius of 511.98 km and is cut there. The dwell counts `not held: D26` once, and each detection outside the cut area is left out and counted (`beyond reach`), so a packet never reports a target outside its own area. The depth an output carries beyond the near edge of a sensor report's area is the channel's own statement, `ChannelSpec.maxAreaDepth` in metres (CR-0035; ADR-0037 point 4): `gmti` declares 511 980 m, and the simulator warns, and writes its relation kind `simulator.beyondReach`, for the tracks farther from the platform than the area's inner radius plus that depth, exactly the ones `gmti` leaves out.

### 4.3 `gmti` out: dwells from the simulator's sensor

`gmti` offers output channels that take `SENSOR_REPORTS` only (ADR-0048 points 4 and 5; `ChannelSpec.kinds`). It receives `ChannelItem.SensorReport(sensorId, platform, time, area, detections, sensor)` (renamed from `ChannelItem.Dwell` by the owner, KP, 2026-11-08; 4607's own dwells keep their name), where each `Detection(objectId, content)` is the detected object's content with the measured position as its `Point` (owner, Q7; SDK 1.4.0 part 1 on `integration/sdk-v1.4`), and the nullable `sensor` is the sensor's description and settings (CR-0033, CR-0034). The inbox keeps the latest sensor report per sensor, so a slow link loses whole reports, which the counters count (review R7).

Per **sensor report** the link sends one packet:

- **Packet header:** `P1` by the link's setting `edition` (M9.1; owner, 2026-11-20): `edition-3` writes "30" and is the default, also for links set up before M9.1, because the receivers in use expect Edition 3; `edition-4` writes "41". The two editions lay out every field alike, and what `gmti` writes means the same in both, except `P10` below (`plugins/gmti/FORMAT-RULES.md`, G18); `P3` the link's nationality setting (default "XN"); `P4` from the highest label among the item's detections and the sensor, mapped back to 1–5 (ADR-0037 point 8 for the limit); `P5` the link's scheme (default "XN"); `P6` 0; `P7` 1 (operation, simulated) or 129 when the sensor's scenario is an exercise, a link setting; `P8` the sensor's name, 10 BCS characters, padded, unique per sensor on the link; `P9` a number derived from the sensor's mission text when it has one (CR-0034: the same mission keeps its id across restarts and links; the `gmti` brief names the function), else a number fixed when the link starts; `P10` the job id, one per sensor (a counter from 1); with `edition-4`, 0 in the packets of the mission and the job definition when they are sent apart from the dwells (AEDP 3.1.10; Edition 3 allows the job id there).
- **Mission segment** in every packet (44 bytes, cheap, and the guide's preference): `M1` the sensor's mission (CR-0034: a simulator setting of 1 to 64 characters, cut to 12 and counted, characters outside BCS replaced as for `P8`; all spaces when there is none), `M2` spaces, `M3` 255 or the sensor's platform type setting, `M4` "emcc" and the version, `M5`–`M7` the **link's reference day**, fixed when the link starts; a link that runs past midnight keeps the day and lets `D6` pass 86 400 000, as the format allows.
- **Job definition** before the sensor's first dwell and then at least every 20 s per sensor (M9.1, owner ADQ B: sent at 30 s with the next dwell, it reached receivers more than 30 s apart, over the standard's limit): `J1` the job id; `J2` 255 or a setting; `J3` "SIM"; `J4` 0; `J5` 1 (255 when the sensor is removed); `J6`–`J13` the sensor's bounding area, clockwise, from its settings (the area it is set to watch; for a moving sector, the box around the current area); `J14` 1 (MTI); `J15` the revisit time in deciseconds; `J16`–`J20` no statement or the sensor's platform error settings; `J21` the range error in cm, `J22` the cross-range error as an angle (atan of the cross-range error over the range to the area's centre), `J23` the velocity error or no statement, `J24` the minimum detectable velocity in dm/s, `J25` the detection probability in percent, `J26` no statement; `J27` 0, `J28` 0 (the simulator's heights are the tracks' own).
- **Dwell segment:** `D2` the sensor's revisit counter (incremented per revisit, wrapping at 65 535), `D3` 0, `D4` 1 (one dwell per revisit, section 3.2); `D5` the detections, split into several segments of the same indices when they exceed the link's cap per segment (default 120, section 3.7); `D6` from the item's `time` and the reference day; `D7`–`D9` from the item's `platform` (longitude first in the SDK, latitude first in the bytes; altitude × 100); `D15`–`D17` when the simulator has the platform's track and speed; `D24`–`D27` from the item's `area` (section 4.2); `D31` the sensor's minimum detectable velocity. Reports: `D32.1` 0, 1, 2…; `D32.2`/`D32.3` full precision (no scale factors in M5); `D32.6` when the content has an altitude; `D32.7` the radial speed of the detected object relative to the sensor when the content has speed and course, else absent (and then no `D32.8`); `D32.10` the **simulated** class (128–255) from the content's symbol: ground vehicle → 130 wheeled or 129 tracked by the symbol's entity, rotary wing 131, fixed wing 132, surface vessel 134, dismount 137, else 255 unknown; `D32.11` 100; `D32.12`–`D32.15` the sensor's error settings (needs `D12`–`D14` in the body: the sensor's own position errors, 0 when it has none). Truth tags are not sent in M5.
- **No HRR, free text, status, history or platform location.** A sensor whose platform moves between revisits sends nothing in between; its position in emc2 is the ground truth object.

### 4.5 What M5 leaves out

HRR and range-Doppler data; job request and acknowledge (no tasking); processing history; free text and status beyond counters; controlled extensions; the reduced-bandwidth positions when sending (reading them is in); embedding in NSIF, NITF or STANAG 7023 and the XML form (Guide Annex B, F question 12); relaying received GMTI out (ADR-0048 point 5); mapping `P6` code words to label categories; heights relative to a geoid; associating detections into tracks.

## 5. Transport

**What the documents say.** The format is a presentation-layer format and leaves the layers below to other agreements (Guide 1.2, D.1). It assumes the transport delivers each packet whole and error-free or not at all, in order, without telling anyone about losses (Guide D.3). Where the transport is worse, the guide suggests a layer that adds sequence numbers, a channel or type tag when the stream is shared with other data, and a sub-packet scheme when the transport's packet size is smaller than the GMTI packet (Guide D.4–D.6). The guide's reference library reads "a buffer read from disk or received from a TCP/UDP stream" (Guide I.2), and the FAQ names UHF SATCOM and Ethernet (Guide F, question 2). Packets may also be wrapped in NSIF/NITF files or STANAG 7023 streams (Guide Annex B); emc2 does not.

**In practice, and for emc2's links** (ADR-0036 connection tasks):

- **TCP:** a byte stream of packets back to back. Framing is `P2`: read 32 bytes, read `P2 − 32` more, parse, repeat. A sender writes each packet whole. A reader that loses sync has no marker to find; it reconnects (section 3.6). The emc2 link runs as a client or a server (ADR-0048 point 2); a server binds to loopback unless the admin chooses otherwise (CLAUDE.md section 4).
- **UDP:** one packet per datagram, so a packet must fit: at most 65 507 bytes of payload on IPv4, and in practice below the path MTU (about 1 400 bytes) to avoid fragmentation and loss. After the headers and a mandatory-only body (80 bytes), 1 400 bytes hold about 130 reports of index plus full-precision position (10 bytes), 70 of the targeting class (19 bytes) or 220 reduced ones (6 bytes); larger dwells are split as section 1.5 allows. The emc2 UDP link accepts a datagram as one packet and drops any whose `P2` differs from the datagram's length. Multicast is a UDP detail the brief may add.
- **Files:** a recording is packets concatenated, exactly as a TCP stream would carry them; the ".4607" extension is customary, not standard. The same `P2` framing reads it.

**The ESA's playback** (ADR-0048 point 8: the owner's recorded open data, approved by the owner, licence in the service's sheet) needs:

- a player that reads a file of concatenated packets, frames them by `P2`, and sends them over TCP (as a server the emc2 link connects to, or a client connecting to the emc2 link's server) or UDP to a host and port, both outside emc2 and started with the sheet's commands (CLAUDE.md section 8);
- **pacing by the recorded time:** send each packet when its first dwell's `D6` is due relative to the previous one, with a speed factor and a "flat out" option; packets without dwells go with the next dwell;
- **a loop option** that replays from the start when the file ends; the mission segment's reference day is then in the past twice over, which brings the time rule below;
- **time handling:** the recording's reference day and dwell times are historical. Either the player rewrites `M5`–`M7` and every `D6`, `T4`, `L1` to "now" (a byte-level edit, since the fields are fixed-size; longitudes and the rest stay), or the emc2 link has a setting "time: as recorded | received", and the ITA's test uses "received" so that detections do not expire at once. The ACA recommends the link setting, and the rewrite as a later option of the player;
- the recording's **version id** and classification: the player passes them through; the emc2 link reads every version (section 3.6) and maps the classification as section 4.1, with the setting below for the AFSERS files' `P4` = 2;
- the ESA's sheet records the data's source, licence, which segments and fields it uses (a histogram of `S1` values and of the dwell mask bits), its reference day, duration, platform ids and the count of dwells and reports, so that the ITA can assert on them. Section 5.3 has the ACA's counts as a start.

What the recordings teach, beyond the standard:

- **No "41" anywhere.** Every file is Edition 2.1 or 3.0. Section 3.6's rule is not optional.
- **The mask rules the layout; the conditions do not** (simulator set A, VISTA). A parser that refuses `D32.12` without `D12` rejects two of the five sets.
- **Multi-day `D6`** (AFSERS pcap: 2 days 13 h) and **a reference day that changes mid-stream** (one AFSERS `.4607` file): keep the latest mission segment per platform and apply it to later dwells only.
- **Job ids churn** (simulator set B): key the area by platform (section 4.1).
- **Large dwells** (531 reports, 15 KB packets) and **dense streams** (10 dwells a second, 2 600 reports a second) make the cap per dwell and the data type's expiry real limits: 4.7 million detections in 30 minutes cannot all live in the store. The ITA uses this file for load, with a cap.
- **The AFSERS `.4607` files say `P4` = 2** while the pcaps of the same system say 5; all are simulated (`P7` = 1). **Owner, 2026-11-05:** the files are unclassified test data; their markings are set to SECRET only to test a system's classification handling. So the emc2 link needs a setting for what to do with a packet's classification: map it (default), or ignore it and label everything with the link's default label. The ITA tests both with these files: mapping, to see SECRET objects refused by or placed in the right layer; ignoring, for ordinary runs. The ESA's sheet records the owner's statement.
- **Platform ids are 10 characters:** a sender's 11-character name arrives cut to its first 10. Names are cut, not rejected.
- **Segments 12 and 102 appear in real streams**, and a free text once: skipping must be routine, not an error path.
- **UDP and multicast** are what the recorded systems used: unicast, and two multicast groups. The `gmti` UDP link should join a multicast group when the admin gives one (an open point for the brief, now with a reason).

**The tools in the folder:**

- **The owner's GMTI dissector** (PowerShell): listens on UDP unicast or multicast, decodes the packet header, mission, dwell, job definition and platform location segments to a log, and can record the raw stream to a `.4607` file or decode a file. It is a second opinion for the ITA and a recorder for the ESA. Two caveats from reading it: it decodes **signed binary angles as sign-and-magnitude**, so negative latitudes, pitches and rolls come out wrong (its own comment doubts the standard's `SA16 0xCE66` example, which is right: two's complement, section 1.1; northern latitudes decode the same either way, which is why the owner's data looks right); and it needs a mission segment before it shows times, as any receiver does.
- **A third-party pcap replayer** (Windows): replays a pcap keeping the recorded spacing, rewriting MAC and IP addresses from its settings file. It is one way for the ESA's playback of the pcap sets; the `.4607` files need a player that frames by `P2` (section 5), which the ESA writes or finds, with the owner's approval of anything installed.

## 6. Pitfalls and a test checklist

### 6.1 Pitfalls

1. **Latitude first in the bytes, longitude first in emc2.** Every field pair in the format is latitude then longitude.
2. **Longitude 0–360.** A western longitude decodes above 180; a negative one must be wrapped before encoding. A `BA32` never holds a negative value.
3. **Two angle encodings.** `BA` is unsigned over 360°, `SA` is signed over 180°; a latitude read as `BA32` is wrong by a factor of two and in sign. `D22`/`D23` and `D29`/`D30` are `SA16`; `D15`, `D21`, `D27`, `D28` are `BA16`.
4. **The existence mask is the layout.** Compute offsets from the mask, never from a fixed struct. The report bits mean nothing when `D5` is 0. Spare bits may be set by careless producers; ignore them.
5. **Guide example masks:** the targeting-and-tracking mask in Guide Table C-3 does not match its own field list when read against AEDP Figure 3-1 (by the ACA's reading, bytes 4 and 3 should be `0xFB 0x9F` for that list). Derive masks from the figure, not from the examples.
6. **Mixed units.** Centimetres (sensor altitude, slant range error), decimetres (cross range error, vertical velocity, MDV), millimetres per second (sensor speed), kilometres (range half extent), half and quarter decibels. `D17` is dm/s but its error `D20` is cm/s. `J22` is an angle while `D32.13` is a length.
7. **`D9` is centimetres as `S32`:** 10 km is 1 000 000. `D32.6` is metres as `S16`: ±32 km.
8. **`B16` is sign-magnitude,** not two's complement: −3.25 km is `0x81A0`, not `0xFE60`.
9. **No reference day, no time.** Buffer or drop until a mission segment arrives; the playback's first dwell may arrive before its mission segment.
10. **`D6` may exceed a day** and the reference day is the mission's, not today's. A dwell at 117 935 200 ms is on the day after the reference day.
11. **Job 0 packets carry no dwells;** a dwell in a job-0 packet is malformed.
12. **One job, one mode:** a mode change is a new job id and a new job definition.
13. **Empty dwells matter:** they move the platform and the area and say the area was looked at. Do not drop them.
14. **Reports can be split across segments and packets** with the same indices; the cap applies to the dwell, not the segment.
15. **Simulated targets use the upper classification range** (128–255) and the exercise indicator says simulated; a reader that only knows 0–8 shows them as unknown.
16. **`P4` has no "none";** an unclassified packet says 5. A label mapping that expects 0 fails on every packet.
17. **Older recordings** say "20" or "30" in `P1` and lack `D32.18` and the newer classes; the mask handles the field, the enumeration rule handles the values.
18. **Resynchronising a TCP stream is impossible** without a marker; reconnect.
19. **UDP size:** a 4 KB dwell packet in one datagram fragments and is lost on many networks; split dwells or use TCP.
20. **The dwell area is not the bounding area:** the bounding area is the task, the dwell area is what was looked at (Annex C-1.13). Draw the dwell area.
21. **Radial speed is along the line of sight,** positive away from the sensor, possibly aliased by `2 × D32.8`; it is not the target's speed.
22. **Floating point and 32-bit integers:** a `BA32` near 360° rounds to 2^32 and must wrap to 0; use `& 0xFFFFFFFF` or `mod 360` before encoding.
23. **`SA` is two's complement, not a sign bit plus magnitude.** A decoder that reads the top bit as a flag and the rest as 45°, 22.5°… is right for positive angles and wrong for every negative one (the owner's dissector does this, section 5.3). Test with a southern latitude.
24. **The conditions on `C` fields are not honoured by real senders** (section 5.3): parse by the mask alone.
25. **The reference day can change during a stream,** and `D6` can run days past it. Never cache the day per link; cache it per platform and mission, and replace it when a new mission segment arrives.
26. **A dense sender overwhelms a naive store:** 2 600 reports a second in simulator set B's file. The cap per dwell, the expiry and the inbox's "latest dwell per sensor" rule exist for this.

### 6.2 Test checklist (for the PCA's unit tests and the ITA)

Encoders and decoders, each with a point where latitude ≠ longitude (ADR-0007):

- Stockholm 59.3293 N, 18.0686 E: latitude `SA32 0x54612390`, longitude `BA32 0x0CD949CB`; decode back within 1e−6°.
- Cape Town 33.9249 S, 18.4241 E: `SA32 0xCFC05223`, `BA32 0x0D1A014B`.
- Rio de Janeiro 22.9068 S, 43.1729 W: `SA32 0xDF6BE37E`, longitude 316.8271° E = `BA32 0xE14C9D08`, decoding to −43.1729 in emc2.
- The standard's own: `BA16 0x591C` = 125.31006°; −34.876099° = `SA16 0xCE66` (Annex C-4.6, C-4.7).
- `B16`: 12.5 km = `0x0640`; −3.25 = `0x81A0`.
- `BA16` 7.5° = `0x0555` (a dwell angle half extent).
- Time: 12:34:56.789 on the reference day = 45 296 789 ms; 08:45:35.2 on the next day = 117 935 200 ms (Annex C-3).
- The reduced position form: `D24`/`D25` with scale factors and deltas, checked against the hi-res form of the same point, including a dwell centred west of the prime meridian with a target east of it.

Parsing:

- A packet with mandatory fields only: mask `0xFF 00 03 C0 00 00 00 00`, body 43 bytes, `D5` 0, packet size 32 + 5 + 43 = 80.
- The same with 3 hi-res reports and `D32.1`: report mask bits `D32.1`, `D32.2`, `D32.3`; 10 bytes each.
- The report bits set while `D5` = 0: no report bytes read.
- A multi-segment packet: mission, job definition, dwell; and one dwell per packet.
- A dwell before any mission segment: held or dropped and counted; after the mission segment arrives, placed on the right day.
- A dwell with `D6` past 86 400 000: the next day.
- A packet with an unknown segment type 128 and a reasonable `S2`: skipped, counted, the following segment parsed.
- A malformed packet (`S2` past `P2`): dropped, counted, the link stays up; over TCP, the connection task reconnects.
- Version "30" packets parse as "41".
- Classification 5 → UNCLASSIFIED, 4 → RESTRICTED, 1–3 above the link's layer → the brief's rule; `P6` kept.
- Exercise indicator 129 → exercise context; 1 → simulation.
- The cap per dwell: a dwell of 500 reports with a cap of 120 writes 120 and counts 380 skipped, across two segments of the same dwell.
- The dwell area: a sensor at a known point, a centre 30 km away at bearing 90°, `D26` 5 km, `D27` 7.5°: an `ArcBand` of radii 25 000 and 35 000 m, bearings 82.5° to 97.5°; and the reverse.

With the owner's recordings (section 5.3), as unit fixtures or through the playback:

- The VISTA stream as the dissector recorded it (a unit fixture; the ESA's playback serves the pcap on port 8113, which holds the whole stream: 186 packets, 85 dwells, 528 reports, at most 18 per dwell): 71 packets, 32 dwells, 297 reports, all class 138, `D6` from 79 349 875 ms (22:02:29.875 UTC); the dwell area decodes to a half disc of 210.7 km around a sensor at 9 147 m north of the equator and east of Greenwich, with latitude ≠ longitude.
- Simulator set A's file: version "21", 34 686 dwells, 33 259 reports, report errors without dwell errors, a western longitude stored above 180° (as degrees east) decoding to the negative longitude in emc2.
- The AFSERS `.4607` file whose reference day changes: `P4` = 2 handled by the link's classification setting, both ways (owner: unclassified test data marked SECRET on purpose); the reference day changes once; 60 processing history, 4 job acknowledge and 2 999 platform location segments skipped or applied; two platform ids.
- The larger AFSERS pcap: 5 099 datagrams at 4 per second, `D6` two days after the reference day.
- Simulator set B's file, for load: 17 991 dwells in 30 minutes with a cap of 120 reports per dwell writes at most 2.16 million detections and counts the rest as skipped; the store stays responsive with the data type's expiry.

End to end (ITA, ADR-0048's consequences):

- `gmti` in from the ESA's playback: the platform symbol moves, the area follows, detections appear with the packet's classification and fade by the data type's defaults; the counters match the sheet's counts.
- The simulator's sensor out through `gmti` into a second emc2 link (or the ESA's reader): the detections come back at the measured positions, the classification is in the simulated range, `D6` and the reference day give the dwell's time within a second.
- The same sensor out through `cot`: each detection as an ordinary position of the detected object.
- A UDP link with a dwell too large for one datagram: split, all reports received.
- A link whose packet has no mapping for its classification: the default label, counted.
